# Mapping 583 million people for a 1590 start

On 1 January 1590 the world held roughly **583 million people** (about **589 million by 1600**), and two places decide most of any honest map: Ming China at about **175 million** inside its registered territory and the Indian subcontinent at about **142 million**, together more than half of humanity. Europe including all of Russia adds about 97 million, sub-Saharan Africa about 58 million, the Middle East, North Africa, Caucasus and Central Asia about 38 million, South-East Asia about 24 million, the Americas only about 12 million after the epidemic collapse, and Oceania about 3 million. These totals are built bottom-up from 205 country rows in present-day borders; if every row sat at its low or its high bound the world would run from about 475 to 713 million, and a coherent "Maddison-lineage" world sits near 533 million against a high-variant world near 640 million. Firm ground exists only where historical demography exists: Malanima's Italy, the English inverse projection, the 1591 Castilian census, Malta's 1590 count, Habib's India, Saito and Takashima's Japan, Reid's South-East Asia, Cook and Borah's central Mexico and N. D. Cook's Peru. Almost everything else is a Maddison/Avakov or HYDE compilation or a labelled placeholder, and every row below carries an A–D grade so the weak ones can be tuned without guilt. Sub-national evidence is thinner still: China (Cao Shuji's 1630 provinces), India (the 1901 census scaled to Habib), Japan (the 1598 kokudaka), Korea (later registers), Italy, Spain and Peru have evidence-based splits, and the rest are allocations. Beijing and Istanbul, at about 700,000 each, head a list of 96 cities with citable figures plus 59 tier-only candidates, several of which need dated 1590 adjustments because the decade was unusually violent.

## Grades, years and borders: how to read every table

Every figure is in **thousands of people within present-day borders**, keyed to ISO 3166-1 codes for countries and ISO 3166-2 codes for first-level divisions, so the tables can be joined to a modern map and then re-aggregated into 1590 polities. Each row carries a **c.1600 best estimate**, the benchmark most historical demography reports, and a **1 January 1590 start value**, which equals the c.1600 figure unless dated evidence moves it. The decade matters in several places. England's reconstructed population rose from 3.94 million in 1591 to 4.16 million in 1601 ([Munro](https://www.economics.utoronto.ca/munro5/14POPERMTables.doc)), and Italy's Centre-North from 7.50 to 7.83 million between 1590 and 1600 ([Malanima](https://www.mulino.it/edizioni/volumi/espansioni/malanima/appendice_malanima.pdf)). Peru's Indian population fell from 968,197 to 851,994 over the same decade ([BCRP, N. D. Cook series](https://estadisticas.bcrp.gob.pe/estadisticas/series/documentos/Anexo-CHE-Tomo-II-597.pdf)), Korea lost roughly a fifth of its people in the 1592–98 Imjin War ([Michell summary](https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/nh/view.do?levelId=nh_030_0050_0030_0010_0030)), and Malta's count of 28,864 in 1590 had fallen to 21,102 by 1595 after famine and plague ([Geneanum](https://en.geneanum.com/malta/documents/history/the-population-of-malta-in-the-seventeenth-century-a-reflection-of-modernity.html)).

Confidence grades are harmonised across regions. **A** marks a count, census or national demographic reconstruction for that unit and date, plus islands known to be uninhabited. **B** marks one explicit scholarly estimate, or a compilation value cross-checked against another source. **C** marks a compilation value with no independent check, or a value split or interpolated from a B-or-better total by a stated method. **D** marks a modelled placeholder or a correction with no direct numeric source; treat every D row as a tunable game parameter. The Americas research used a three-step scale, so its interpolated rows appear here as C and its working estimates as D. City rows use H/M/L/U instead (census-backed, one compilation, disputed, no figure). A row's range is the spread of published or derived figures for that unit; a region's total range is the sum of every low and every high bound, an envelope that overstates the real uncertainty because the errors do not all run the same way. Source columns carry short IDs that resolve in the source register at the end.

Two numbers decide most of the map. Ming China carries about 175 million in 1590, the overlap point between the Western syntheses (Maddison's 162 million peak in 1580–90, Fairbank's 160 million, Brook's 175 million) and the Fudan reconstructions (Ge Jianxiong's 197 million for 1600, Cao Shuji's 192.5 million peak in 1630) ([zh.wikipedia, 中国人口史](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E4%B8%AD%E5%9B%BD%E4%BA%BA%E5%8F%A3%E5%8F%B2); [Wikipedia, Population history of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_history_of_China)). The Indian subcontinent carries Irfan Habib's **142 million**, cross-checked three ways (cultivated area 142 million, land revenue 144.3 million, army sizes 140–150 million) and adopted by Broadberry and Gupta ([Broadberry & Gupta](https://web.williams.edu/Economics/seminars/BroadberryGuptaIndiaAndTheGreatDivergence.pdf)). A 15% swing in China alone moves the world by more than the Americas and Oceania combined. Asia as a whole, counting Anatolia, Iran, Arabia, the Caucasus, Central Asia and Siberia, holds about 69% of the 1590 total.

The bottom-up total runs above the Maddison lineage because each regional study found reasons to prefer newer national scholarship: Habib over Maddison's 135 million for India ([Wikipedia, Economic history of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_history_of_India)), HYDE 3.3's 58 million over Maddison's 44.3 million for sub-Saharan Africa ([Wikipedia, Estimates of historical world population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estimates_of_historical_world_population)), and Saito and Takashima's 17 million over Hayami's 12 million for Japan ([Saito & Takashima](https://risk.ier.hit-u.ac.jp/Japanese/pdf/dp15-3_rcesr.pdf)). The pattern matters for a designer: the compilations are smooth, round-number series largely descended from McEvedy and Jones, whose atlas has been criticised for estimates that are "little more than guesses" ([Guinnane, JEH](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-economic-history/article/we-do-not-know-the-population-of-every-country-in-the-world-for-the-past-two-thousand-years/D747DDC6E499C799B0471DBE33FEB0BB)). ## National totals: 205 rows, only a handful built on real counts

### Europe: national demography for Italy, England, Castile and Malta, compilations elsewhere

Europe excluding Russia sums to about 91.3 million for c.1600, within 3% of Biraben's 89 million ([Malanima](https://www.mulino.it/edizioni/volumi/espansioni/malanima/appendice_malanima.pdf)) and of the ~92 million implied by OWID's 101.5 million for Europe including 9.5 million in Russia ([OWID via de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_der_L%C3%A4nder_und_Territorien_nach_Bev%C3%B6lkerungsentwicklung)). Where national demography exists it beats the compilations: Italy follows Malanima's Beloch-based series, England the inverse projection, Spain the 1591 census, Malta its 1590 count. Most of the Balkans, the Baltic, Belarus, Ukraine and the micro-states rest on Avakov's modern-border compilation, which its own Wikipedia page warns is "likely inaccurate for the period" and which is internally inconsistent in places (Sweden's and Denmark–Norway's totals do not equal their sub-entries) ([Wikipedia 1600 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600)). Russia is split into a European part and an Asian part so that the Urals frontier can be modelled separately. Nine micro-states and small territories have no source at all and carry density placeholders.

### Middle East, North Africa, Caucasus and Central Asia: Avakov's grid with McEvedy and Jones corrections

The only complete modern-border series for this region is Avakov's, which gives Turkey 7,275k, Iran 4,472k, Yemen 2,243k and Egypt 5,000k for 1600 ([Wikipedia, Demographics of the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_the_Middle_East)). McEvedy and Jones correct it in two important places: they treat 5 million as a ceiling for Egypt "not exceeded until modern times", with stagnation periods "nearer 3m than 5m" and about 3.5 million in 1800, and they divide the Maghreb on a 2:2:1 formula among Morocco, Algeria and Tunisia ([McEvedy & Jones](https://dmo.econ.msu.ru/Teaching/Histpop/Reading/Atlas%20of%20World%20Pop%20History%20McEvedy&Jones.pdf)). The same atlas calls the traditional 5 million for medieval Georgia "absurd" and finds "nothing at all substantial" for Central Asia before the Russian conquest, which is why the Caucasus, Afghanistan and the Central Asian republics are grade D. Iran in present borders sits at 5.5 million because 1590 followed a decade of Ottoman and Uzbek wars; the whole Safavid realm reached 8–10 million only by 1650 ([Blake](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/time-in-early-modern-islam/safavid-mughal-and-ottoman-empires/9D55F0A0262017473EC8A9A7ED86C508)) and was "never higher than nine million" in the 17th century ([Floor via BIPS](https://www.bips.ac.uk/?p=3734)). These rows use the same value for 1590 and c.1600.

### South Asia: Habib's 142 million split by the 1901 census

The subcontinent total is Habib's 142 million for 1600, close to Moosvi's 145 million for 1601 (her two methods gave 136–150 million) ([Moosvi, OUP](https://academic.oup.com/book/32718/chapter-abstract/272400288)). The split between India, Pakistan and Bangladesh applies regional 1600/1901 ratios to each present-day unit's 1901 census population, so on Habib's numbers c.1600 is roughly half of 1901; Moreland independently judged the south c.1600 at "about half" of 1911 ([Moreland 1920](https://archive.org/stream/cu31924022895001/cu31924022895001_djvu.txt)). Bangladesh gets a lower ratio (0.40) because the eastern delta was still an agrarian frontier. Nepal follows Avakov (2.75 million); Bhutan, Sri Lanka and the Maldives have no scholarly c.1600 figure. Use these values unchanged for 1590: long-run growth of about 0.2% a year and the 1596 famine roughly cancel over one decade.

### East Asia: real population, not the Ming registers

The Ming registers recorded 60,692,856 persons in 1578 and 56,305,050 in 1602 ([zh.wikipedia, 中国人口史](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E4%B8%AD%E5%9B%BD%E4%BA%BA%E5%8F%A3%E5%8F%B2)). These measure fiscal households, and Brook attributes the shortfall to families under-reporting members (especially daughters) and officials under-reporting households ([Wikipedia, Population history of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_history_of_China)); no modern scholar uses them as a headcount, but at about one-third of the real population they make a ready-made "tax-registered" figure for the game's fiscal model. China's row adds about 2.85 million for frontier peoples outside Ming registration (Jurchens, Mongols, Tibetans, Xinjiang), all grade D. Japan follows Saito and Takashima's 17.0 million for the surveyed area in 1600 plus about 100k for Ryukyu (108,958 in 1632) and 25k for Ezo ([Wikipedia, Demographics of Japan before Meiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Japan_before_Meiji_Restoration)). Korea's 11.0 million for 1590 lies between Han Young-woo's 9.58 million and Kwon and Shin's 14.04 million ([우리역사넷](https://contents.history.go.kr/front/nh/view.do?levelId=nh_033_0020_0010_0010)); Kwon and Shin's own series gives 11.72 million for 1600 ([동북아역사넷](https://contents.nahf.or.kr/item/level.do?levelId=edeah.d_0004_0030_0020_0030)), the figure Wikipedia attributes to Maddison.

### South-East Asia and Oceania: Reid's 23 million and contact-era back-projections

Reid's table of 1600 estimates, a total of **23 million** at about 5.5 people per km², is the most defensible base for South-East Asia: it is region-by-region, anchored on dated counts (Philippine tributes 1591, Bali 1597, Kedah 1614, Mataram 1631, Siam 1687) and back-projected from 1800 at a stated 0.2% a year ([Reid](https://archive.org/details/southeast-asia-in-the-age-of-commerce-1450-1680-the-lands-below-the-winds)). Newson's archival reconstruction supersedes Reid for the Philippines, with Luzon at 683,760 and the Visayas at 221,700 in 1600 and a decline still under way from about 1.57 million in 1565 ([review of Newson](https://thezoomerhistorian.medium.com/a-review-of-conquest-and-pestilence-in-the-early-spanish-philippines-fa5a60504398); [Newson, MUSE](https://muse.jhu.edu/book/8333)). Every Pacific figure is a contact-era estimate (1767–1860) assumed flat back to 1600, and recent archaeology keeps pushing them up: lidar puts pre-contact Tonga at 100–120k ([Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2022-09-reveals-severe-impact-european-contact.html)), and a 2026 study puts Aboriginal Australia at a median 2.22 million in 1788 (range about 1–4 million), against the long-standing 315k minimum and 750k "sustainable" figures ([UNSW](https://www.unsw.edu.au/newsroom/news/2026/09/more-than-2-million-indigenous-people-Australia-1788); [ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/ausstats/abs@.nsf/bb8db737e2af84b8ca2571780015701e/68ae74ed632e17a6ca2573d200110075!OpenDocument)). Papua New Guinea has no scholarly figure at all. Hawaiʻi is listed under the United States in the Americas table.

### Sub-Saharan Africa: HYDE 3.3 with eight anomalies repaired

The only complete present-day-border series for Africa before 1800 is HYDE 3.3, shown through Our World in Data ([OWID](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population?tab=table&time=1600&region=Africa)). It is a top-down allocation with visible errors: Benin at 3.1 million (more than its 1950 population of 2.25 million), Sierra Leone at 7k, Gambia at 4k, and Ethiopia flat at 2.355 million from 1500 to 1700 ([OWID 1500–1700](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population?tab=table&time=1500..1700)). Those rows are replaced by values based on each country's ratio to its 1950 population. Repairing Benin (−2.45 million) roughly offsets raising Ethiopia (+2.1 million), so the corrected total of 58.5 million stays almost exactly at HYDE's own 58.4 million, about a third above Maddison's 44.3 million. Kongo's best documentary estimate is Thornton's: about 509,000 in 1650 from Capuchin baptism data, later revised to about 790,000, against an older consensus of 2 million ([Thornton 1977](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-african-history/article/abs/demography-and-history-in-the-kingdom-of-kongo-15501750/DDA0D0AD06657B5C47D10952B7B4585B); [Thornton revision](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-african-history/article/revising-the-population-history-of-the-kingdom-of-kongo/7CF3BDE8A2CB2D4EE26BD79644143A8F)). Mauritius (settled 1638), Seychelles (1770) and Saint Helena (1659) were uninhabited in 1590 ([History of Mauritius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Mauritius); [History of Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Seychelles); [WASC](https://wasc.org.uk/WASC_hist_StH.html)). The 1590 column backs each country off at HYDE's own 1500–1600 growth rate, capped at 0.4% a year.

### The Americas: archival series for the Spanish core, guesses for the rest

The Americas were near, but not yet at, their demographic nadir in 1590. Where Spanish tribute and census records exist the figures are solid: Cook and Borah's **1.375 million** Indians in central Mexico in 1595 ([Cook & Borah](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/essays-in-population-history-vol-iii-mexico-and-california.html)), N. D. Cook's 968,197 Indians in colonial Peru in 1590 ([BCRP](https://estadisticas.bcrp.gob.pe/estadisticas/series/documentos/Anexo-CHE-Tomo-II-597.pdf)), and the Central American series compiled by Lovell and Lutz from Gerhard, Newson, Fowler and Quirós ([Lovell & Lutz](https://clagscholar.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/lovell6.pdf)). Brazil's Portuguese colonial sphere held only about 60,000 people in 1585 and at least 80,000 by c.1612 ([Carrara](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/1670/167031535019.pdf)); the 1.5 million independent natives around it are a placeholder. North of Mexico the table uses mid-range contact estimates (Snow, Milner, Denevan: 3.4–3.8 million for the US and Canada) with modest pre-1600 losses, rejecting Dobyns's 18 million, which most scholars consider excessive ([NAP, Thornton](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9599/chapter/6); [Wikipedia, Population history of the Indigenous peoples of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_history_of_the_Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas)). The hemisphere sums to about 11.7 million including Hawaiʻi, well above a 2019 model's "about 6 million by 1600" but consistent with that article's "lowest estimate" of about 9 million for Latin America in 1650.

## Inside the giants: sub-national splits from Cao's provinces to placeholder shares

Only seven of the requested countries have splits grounded in period or near-period evidence: China, India, Japan, Korea, Italy, Spain and Peru. Mexico, Brazil, the British Isles and Indonesia have evidence for part of the country. Everything else is a reasoned allocation from the 1590 polity map, later census patterns and ecology, graded D and intended as a starting point for tuning. All tables sum to their national row; where the regional research summed slightly above the national figure, a scaled column is provided.

### China by province: Cao Shuji's 1630 shape scaled to 175 million

No accessible source gives Ming population by modern province for c.1590. The best shape is Cao Shuji's 1630 reconstruction, available only as quotations in secondary Chinese articles: Zhejiang 23.60 million, Jiangxi 19.30m, Henan 16.73m, Bei Zhili 10.39m, Fujian 8.80m, Guangdong 7.80m, Sichuan 7.35m, Huguang 15.0m, "Gansu" 3.79m, Guangxi 3.50m, Guizhou 2.50m and Yunnan 2.40m ([wyzxwk](https://www.wyzxwk.com/Article/lishi/2011/05/227216.html); [Zhihu](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/678051614)). The five missing units, marked †, are residuals against Cao's 192.5 million total, allocated by their 1393 and 1542 registered weights ([zh.wikipedia](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E4%B8%AD%E5%9B%BD%E4%BA%BA%E5%8F%A3%E5%8F%B2)) and by Cao's own 1997 estimate of 17.8 million for Bei Zhili plus Shanxi in 1582 ([aisixiang](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/120213.html)). Each Ming figure is scaled by 0.909 to reach 175 million, and splits of one Ming province into several modern ones (Nan Zhili 55/35/10 into Jiangsu, Anhui and Shanghai; Sichuan 65/35 into Sichuan and Chongqing; Bei Zhili 85/10/5 into Hebei, Beijing and Tianjin) are proxies. Uniform scaling probably overstates the fast-growing south-west frontier in 1590 slightly, and a 2025 study warns that some southern regions show minimal growth after 1393 ([Schneider](https://journals.univie.ac.at/index.php/jeacs/article/view/10068)). The single biggest upgrade available is Cao's 2024 English edition, which publishes prefectural estimates for 1580 ([book launch](https://quantitativehistory.org/events/Quantitative-History-Book-Launch-Series-The-Population-History-of-China-1368%E2%80%931953)).

### India, Pakistan and Bangladesh: the 1901 census times regional ratios

Neither Habib nor Moosvi publishes population by subah, so each present-day state's 1901 census population ([StatisticsTimes](https://statisticstimes.com/demographics/india/indian-states-population.php)) is multiplied by a 1600/1901 ratio and the subcontinent is rescaled to 142 million. The ratios rest on Moosvi's finding that cultivated acreage in the United Provinces rose 89% between 1600 and 1910 (a ratio near 0.5) ([Broadberry & Gupta](https://web.williams.edu/Economics/seminars/BroadberryGuptaIndiaAndTheGreatDivergence.pdf)), Moreland's "about half" for the south, Guha's 2–3 million for the Ahom kingdom and Pearson's 250,000 for Portuguese Goa ([Wikipedia 1600 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600)). The method passes one independent test: its total for Akbar's five core subahs (Agra, Delhi, Lahore, Allahabad, Awadh) is about 35.0 million against Moosvi's published 35.63 million ([Ellis](https://www.lse.ac.uk/Economic-History/Assets/Documents/Research/GEHN/GEHNWP14-FE.pdf)). The derived Mughal empire (about 92 million) sits between Broadberry and Gupta's 85 million and Moosvi's 100 million. Bengal is the largest regional uncertainty: an unsourced Wikipedia infobox gives the Bengal Subah 12.9 million, against about 20 million for West Bengal plus Bangladesh here. Pakistan's 1901 provincial baselines were not verified except for the North-West Frontier Province's 2,125,480 ([New Pak Historian](https://newpakhistorian.wordpress.com/2015/04/27/1901-census-of-the-of-the-north-west-frontier-province-of-pakistan/)), and the Bangladesh division split is a judgement that weights the old-settled west up. Several Indian ISO codes changed between 2019 and 2023; verify them before use.

### Japan by prefecture: the 1598 kokudaka as a population proxy

Saito and Takashima set the surveyed area at 17.0 million in 1600 but give no regional breakdown ([Saito & Takashima](https://risk.ier.hit-u.ac.jp/Japanese/pdf/dp15-3_rcesr.pdf)). The 1598 Keichō kokudaka by province, which sums to 18,454,101 koku, is a sound proxy: its eastern share (Tōhoku, Kantō, Tōsan) is 31.1%, against 32.8% in Saito and Takashima's own 1600 output reconstruction ([史料部屋](https://shiryobeya.com/shokuho/keicho3kokudaka.html)). The 1598 survey understates Dewa and Tosa, whose assessments were incomplete ([Chiebukuro summary](https://detail.chiebukuro.yahoo.co.jp/qa/question_detail/q10121766860)), so the adjusted column doubles their koku and renormalises. Kokudaka also leaves out townspeople: Kyoto alone held 300–400k by c.1609 ([Wikipedia, Demographics of Japan before Meiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Japan_before_Meiji_Restoration)), so Kyoto, Osaka and Hyōgo can take a further 300–400k from the other prefectures. Multi-province prefectures use proxy splits, such as Mutsu 12/20/30/38% to Aomori, Iwate, Miyagi and Fukushima, and Musashi 55/35/10% to Saitama, Tokyo and Kanagawa.

### Korea: post-war registers bent back toward the pre-war south

Provincial registers survive in usable form only after the Imjin War. They show the three southern provinces holding 68% of registered people in 1648–57 (when registration was "extremely incomplete"), about 53% in 1669–78 and an 18th–19th-century average of 50.6% ([우리역사넷, 인구의 분포](https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/nh/view.do?levelId=nh_033_0020_0010_0020)). The 1590 shares bend the 1669–78 pattern toward the south, because the north filled up through later settlement, which puts about two-thirds of Joseon's people inside present-day South Korea. For a game that simulates the war, seed 11.0 million and let the war remove 15–20%, in line with Michell's estimate of a loss of about 20% ([Michell summary](https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/nh/view.do?levelId=nh_030_0050_0030_0010_0030)). Jeonbuk and Gangwon changed status in 2023–24, so check their current ISO codes.

### Russia: one cited anchor, the rest modelled

Only the north-west has a cited anchor: the Novgorod land held 500–800k people in the late 15th century, before the devastation of the 1570s ([Wikipedia, Demographic history of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_history_of_Russia)). Siberia's 200k is essentially indigenous; "according to some estimates" its native peoples numbered 160,000 at the start of the 17th century ([Wikipedia, Indigenous peoples of Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_Siberia)). Do not build the Time of Troubles into the 1590 value: Russia regained its 1600 population only about fifty years later.

### Spain and Portugal: the 1591 census with 1530 regional shares

The 1591 census counted households (*vecinos*) in the Crown of Castile, and the household multiplier drives the whole Spanish dispute: Ruiz Martín's reading gives 6,617,251 people for Castile, Lanza García's 5,302,000 at about four per household ([es.wikipedia](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evoluci%C3%B3n_de_la_poblaci%C3%B3n_espa%C3%B1ola_en_la_%C3%A9poca_precensal); [Modernalia](https://www.modernalia.es/items/show/1358)). ### Nigeria and Ethiopia: colonial-census shares and the 1590 polity map

### Mexico, Peru and Brazil: tribute records where the Spanish counted

Central Mexico's 1.375 million Indians in 1595 is Cook and Borah's tribute-based benchmark, which they caution rests on samples "whose extent and ease of interpretation vary considerably" ([Cook & Borah](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/essays-in-population-history-vol-iii-mexico-and-california.html)); the state split inside it, the Yucatán peninsula and the north are allocations, and Chiapas interpolates Gerhard's 125,100 (1550) and 85,000 (1611) ([Lovell & Lutz](https://clagscholar.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/lovell6.pdf)). Peru has the best regional series of any American country, N. D. Cook's six-zone projection from Viceroy Toledo's census and later revisitas; the coast had collapsed to about 15% of the total and the southern sierra held about half ([BCRP](https://estadisticas.bcrp.gob.pe/estadisticas/series/documentos/Anexo-CHE-Tomo-II-597.pdf)). The source prints the 1590 sierra-central figure as "18,092", a typo for 180,983 given the column total. Brazil's colonial population by captaincy comes from Carrara's reading of Anchieta (1585) and Moreno (1612), adjusted toward the later benchmarks ([Carrara](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/1670/167031535019.pdf)).

## Beijing and Istanbul lead 155 cities, but the evidence thins below 150,000

Beijing and Istanbul top every standard list at about 700,000. For Beijing in 1600, Chandler gives 706,000 and Morris 700,000, while Modelski's 1,000,000 is the outlier ([Wikipedia, Largest cities throughout history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_cities_throughout_history)); for Istanbul, Chandler gives 680,000 in 1575 and 700,000 in 1600 ([Wikipedia, Largest European cities in history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_European_cities_in_history)), although household multipliers of 3 to 5 move any Ottoman city figure by about 40% ([Bilecik summary](https://bilecik.academia.edu/ertcam)). Eleven more cities reach 200,000 or more in 1590: Nanjing, Agra, Lahore, Ahmedabad, Bijapur, Kyoto, Hangzhou, Cairo, Paris, Naples and Osaka. Almost every figure traces back to Chandler (1987), de Vries (1984) or Chandler's "Etext" series as reproduced on Wikipedia, and below about 150,000 the evidence thins quickly outside Europe. Europe is the exception: de Vries and Chandler agree within about 15% for Paris, London, Milan, Rome, Lisbon and Seville, and count-based figures exist for Venice (134,871 in 1581), Seville (121,000 in 1597), Bologna (72,395 in 1588) and Florence (59,216 in 1562) ([Wikipedia, Historical urban community sizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_urban_community_sizes)).

The 1590 column interpolates linearly between Chandler's 1575 and 1600 columns where both exist (London's 112k and 187k give 157k), and otherwise applies dated evidence. Edo grew to 60k by 1600 and 150k by c.1609 from a small start ([Wikipedia, Historical urban community sizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_urban_community_sizes); [Wikipedia, Demographics of Japan before Meiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Japan_before_Meiji_Restoration)), so it starts at about 15k. Hyderabad was founded only in 1591–92, five miles from overcrowded Golconda, so the 80k sits at Golconda in 1590 ([Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/hyderabad)). Potosí grew from about 40k in 1571 to "at least 150,000" by 1600 ([Commonplace](https://commonplace.online/article/high-place-potosi/)), and a 1603 description counts at least 120,000 Andeans, 6,000 Europeans and 5,000 Africans ([Wikipedia, Potosí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potos%C3%AD)), so it starts at about 120k. Lima counted about 26,400 in 1614 ([Wikipedia, Demographics of Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Lima)), and Bishop Salazar's 1590 report put Manila's Chinese at 6–7,000 plus about 2,000 visiting traders ([Inquirer](https://opinion.inquirer.net/?p=119368)). Ayutthaya was still recovering from its 1569 sack, when it fell to about 10k ([Wikipedia, Historical urban community sizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_urban_community_sizes)).

The widest disagreements are in Asia. Nanjing ranges from Chandler's 194k to about 1 million in late-Ming memoir testimony, with Cao Shuji's 450k the chosen middle ([Xu, van Leeuwen & van Zanden](https://pure.knaw.nl/ws/files/8633887/Urbanization_in_China_ca._1100_C1900.pdf)). Agra runs from 370k to 700k, and Dyson argues that earlier research "appears to have overstated the size of Mughal cities" ([Dyson](https://academic.oup.com/book/3581/chapter/144861931)), which is why it is shaded below Chandler's 500k. Bijapur is put at "half a million to a million" at its early-17th-century peak ([Wikipedia, Bijapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijapur)), and Mataram ranges from Nagtegaal's 10–20k to Reid's ~800k, which Reid extrapolated from a Dutch claim of 200,000 fighting men ([Wikipedia, Sultan of Mataram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_of_Mataram)). Two widely copied figures should be ignored: a 400k Naples in one Wikipedia table, against Chandler's 224k, and Ollie Bye's 240k for Vijayanagara in 1600 ([JetPunk](https://www.jetpunk.com/user-quizzes/1428462/biggest-cities-by-decade-1600-1690)), a city already in ruins after 1565 ([Wikipedia, Historical urban community sizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_urban_community_sizes)). Gaur (24.87, 88.13) and Vijayanagara/Hampi (15.33, 76.46) should not be seeded as large cities.

Joining the regional tables exposes inconsistencies that a programmer should resolve rather than inherit. Istanbul's city figure exceeds the 600k the regional research gave all of Istanbul province; the Turkey table corrects this. Pegu's ~150k in 1590 cannot fit inside Bago region's 1600 value of 180k, so Myanmar's ~200k add-back for 1590 should go mostly to Bago. Bolivia's placeholder 500k must contain Potosí's ~120k, an urban share near a quarter that the Bolivia figure should probably be raised to accommodate. Kyoto prefecture's kokudaka-based 424k leaves only about 125k outside the city, another sign that the kokudaka understates the Kinai. The Oceania research quoted a subtotal of about 3.9 million, but its own rows sum to 3.53 million including Hawaiʻi (3.23 million without). Italy's, Spain's and European Russia's regional rows summed 1–2% above their national figures, and the tables above carry scaled columns that match.

Some present-day units have no estimate anywhere in the research: Gibraltar; Réunion and Mayotte; Bermuda and the Falklands; Kiribati, the Marshall Islands, Palau, Tuvalu, Nauru, the Cook Islands, Niue, Wallis and Futuna, American Samoa, Tokelau and Pitcairn; and the small Caribbean territories, which share one Lesser Antilles row. All are small, some may have been uninhabited in 1590, and each should be checked before seeding; American Samoa's Tutuila had 3,900 people in 1865, a post-contact floor ([eHRAF](https://ehrafworldcultures.yale.edu/cultures/ou08/summary)). HYDE 3.x and Clio Infra country values, Biraben's country figures and most of McEvedy and Jones's Asian and African country rows were not consulted directly, so their per-country disagreement with the picks above is not quantified.

## Conclusion

The uncertainty in a 1590 world map is concentrated, not spread evenly. China, the Indian subcontinent, sub-Saharan Africa, the Americas and Japan account for about 85% of the ~107 million gap between a low-variant and a high-variant world, while the two dozen micro-states and island groups carrying D-grade placeholders together could not move the total by half a percent. That makes the research priorities unusually clear: Cao Shuji's 1580 prefectural table ([Brill edition](https://quantitativehistory.org/events/Quantitative-History-Book-Launch-Series-The-Population-History-of-China-1368%E2%80%931953)), Moosvi's subah-level data ([OUP](https://academic.oup.com/book/32718/chapter-abstract/272400288)), Manning's 1650 regional tables for Africa ([discussed in Frankema 2019](https://edepot.wur.nl/471136)) and Ubelaker's culture-area estimates for North America ([Smithsonian](https://research.si.edu/publication-details/?id=21238)) would each upgrade millions to tens of millions of people, whereas perfecting Andorra or the Faroe Islands changes nothing. A second, quieter lever is fiscal visibility: the Ming registers (about a third of the real population), Joseon's 4.16 million registered in 1543 against about 11 million real, the Castilian *vecino* counts and the Ottoman tahrir taxpayers give the game a historically grounded "registered" population that can drive taxation and conscription separately from the real headcount.

The start date also matters more than it first appears. The 1590s concentrate an unusual number of demographic shocks into one decade: the Imjin War in Korea, the collapse of Lower Burma and the sack of Pegu, the onset of the Celali crisis in Anatolia, Spain's plague of 1596–1602 followed by the morisco expulsion, the continuing Andean and Mexican decline, and, just beyond, Russia's Time of Troubles. Seeding the map with c.1600 benchmarks would therefore pre-apply losses that the game itself should generate. The 1590 column in this report deliberately excludes them, so the simulation can produce them as events, and the c.1600 column becomes a ready-made validation target for the first ten years of play.
