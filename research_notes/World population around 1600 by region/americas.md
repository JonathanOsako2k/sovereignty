# Population of the Americas c.1590–1600 (native, European and African), by present-day country and region

Scope note for the report writer: figures are in **thousands** and use **present-day borders** unless stated. Every number in a "Cited Findings" list comes from a fetched page or search result. The programmer-ready tables are under the "Inferences" headings. They mix sourced benchmarks (confidence A/B) with my own working estimates where I found no source (confidence C). C rows must not be presented as scholarly figures. Confidence codes used throughout:
- **A**: direct scholarly estimate for c.1590–1600.
- **B**: interpolated or derived arithmetically from sourced benchmarks.
- **C**: my working estimate; no source retrieved in this session.

## 1. National totals c.1600 (present-day borders) for every country in scope

### Takeaway
Only a few countries have a defensible archival-based figure for c.1590–1600:
- central Mexico (Cook & Borah, 1.375 M Indians in 1595);
- Peru (N. D. Cook, 968 k Indians in 1590, 852 k in 1600);
- the Central American provinces (Lovell & Lutz's compilation of Gerhard, Newson, Fowler and Quirós);
- coastal Portuguese Brazil (c.60–100 k colonial-sphere people).

Everything else (US, Canada, Southern Cone, Guianas, Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, Bolivia, most of the Caribbean) has to be interpolated from contact-era estimates. The best-estimate sum for the hemisphere is about 11.4 M: roughly 8.6 M in Latin America and 2.8 M north of Mexico. The single most important caveat is the very low 2019 model-based figure of about 6 M for the whole hemisphere in 1600.

The Brazil figure is unsourced and is plainly colonists only. The list states that its main source (Avakov, who mostly cites Maddison) "calculates populations using modern borders". — [Wikipedia, List of countries by population in 1600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600)
- **Mexico total.** One page states that by 1600 Mexico's population "was down to 2.5 million" but was still the most populous country in the New World. The page's own source is not named; it is probably Maddison or McEvedy and Jones. — [geo-mexico.com](https://geo-mexico.com/?p=4277)
- **Peru and Mexico together.** Ronald Wright's popular synthesis says that by 1600 Peru and Mexico had each fallen to about a million, losses of about 95%. This is a secondary source. — [Goodreads notes on *A Short History of Progress*](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/19273409-a-short-history-of-progress/21990940-henrik-akselsen/45a946be-8627-477d-b67b-1fcf04c47d80)

**Central America** (Lovell & Lutz, "The Historical Demography of Colonial Central America", Table 2, compiling Gerhard, Newson, Fowler, Daugherty, Quirós and Gasco). Source: [Lovell & Lutz, CLAG](https://clagscholar.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/lovell6.pdf).
- **Chiapas (Gerhard).** 1511: 275,000. 1550: 125,100. 1611: 85,000. 1650: 70,000. 1700: 72,000. The nadir came in the 18th century.
- **Soconusco.** 1511: 80,000 (Gerhard) or 90,000 (Gasco). 1550: 7,000. 1570: 4,800 (Gasco). 1611: 6,600. 1650: 4,000.
- **Guatemala excluding Petén.** 1520: 2,000,000 (Lovell, Lutz & Swezey 1984). 1550: 427,850. About 265,000 by 1821.
- **El Salvador.** 1519: 700–800,000 (Fowler). 1524: 400–500,000. 1570: 70,000 (Daugherty). The nadir came in the second half of the 17th century.
- **Honduras (Newson).** 1524: about 800,000, split into 600,000 in the west and centre and 200,000 in the east. The OCR of the table reads "8,000 Total", but Table 1 gives Newson's Honduras figure as 800,000. 1550: 132,000 (32,000 west and centre; 100,000 east). 1700: 47,544 (nadir). 1800: 62,692.
- **Nicaragua including Nicoya (Newson).** 1524: 800,000, of which 600,000 were in the Mesoamerican (Pacific) zone and 200,000 in the South American (Caribbean) zone. 1555: 190,000. 1675–99: 61,106 (22,263 Meso; 38,843 South American zone). 1800: 83,059.
- **Costa Rica.** Contact: 400,000 (Denevan). 1569: 69,875 (Quirós). 1611: 7,168 plus 540 "infidels" (Quirós). 1682: 1,343 (nadir?).
- **Nadir timing.** Guatemala, El Salvador and Nicaragua reached their nadirs in the second half of the 17th century. Honduras, Costa Rica and Chiapas reached theirs in the 18th century.

**Caribbean**
- **Hispaniola, 1606 census.** The census taken after Osorio's 1605–06 "devastations" of the west and north counted 9,648 slaves for the whole island. Juan Bosch notes that colonial counts gave whites as *vecinos*, to be multiplied by about 5, and Black people by head. — [Acento (Bosch)](https://acento.com.do/opinion/bosch-esclavitud-y-racismo-8864172.html); [Wikipedia, Devastations of Osorio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devastations_of_Osorio)
- **Hispaniola, ratio of Black to white.** There were more than 9,600 enslaved Black people in 1606, plus "several additional hundreds if not thousands" of free Black and mulatto people. Black people outnumbered whites by "almost 2:1, possibly higher" in the early 1600s. — [First Blacks in the Americas (CUNY DSI)](https://firstblacks.org/en/?p=2649)

**Colombia (New Granada)**
- **Tunja tributaries (Colmenares).** 61,500 in 1551, implying about 196,800 people. 59,111 in 1562; 42,679 in 1572; 15,811 in 1600, a fall of about 73% between 1562 and 1600. General inspections exist for 1560, 1562, 1571, 1600 and 1636. — [Anuario Colombiano de Historia Social y de la Cultura (search result)](https://revistas.unal.edu.co/index.php/achsc/article/download/36342/37914/151469)
- **New Granada as a whole.** Licenciado Monzón (c.1580) reported about 350,000 "indios encomendados" in the Nuevo Reino. It is disputed whether this counts tributaries or the whole population. — [Revista Universidad Nacional](https://revistas.unal.edu.co/index.php/revistaun/article/download/11753/12443/29629)

**Chile**
- **Mapuche.** José Bengoa estimates 705,000–900,000 Mapuche between the Itata and the Seno de Reloncaví in the mid-16th century. — [Wikipedia, Mapuche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapuche)
- **Decline.** The Spanish found about 1 million people in 16th-century Chile, and about two-thirds of the original population died in the first fifty years of contact (Bengoa 2003). — [USACH Breves](https://fae.usach.cl/fae/docs/brevesPP/Breves08.pdf)
- **A lower figure.** Luis Millones cites Bengoa's "perhaps 500,000" as the pre-Columbian figure. — [El Comercio](https://elcomercio.pe/opinion/columnistas/chile-cuestion-mapuche-luis-millones-noticia-584015)

**North America.** Contact estimates and the Alaska, California and Canada figures are cited in section 5.

**How the B rows were derived**
- **Chiapas.** Linear interpolation of Gerhard's figures between 1550 (125.1 k) and 1611 (85 k) gives about 99 k in 1590 and about 92 k in 1600. Add Soconusco at about 6 k (4.8 k in 1570, 6.6 k in 1611). This gives **MX-CHP ≈ 98 k Indians c.1595**.
- **Guatemala.** Several regional series show 1550→1575 losses of about 35–45%: the Northwest fell 36%, Totonicapán about 82% (1525–1575), and Atitlán about 5%. Zamora's western series shows a further fall of about 15% from 1575 to 1600. Applying roughly ×0.65 then ×0.85 to LLS's 427,850 gives **about 235 k for Guatemala excluding Petén c.1600**. This is well above Zamora's 64 k for the west alone and below Solano's 195 k plus the rest of the country.
- **Honduras.** Exponential interpolation between 132 k (1550) and 47.5 k (1700) gives about 94 k for 1600. Because losses were front-loaded, I use 90 k.
- **Nicaragua.**
  - Pacific zone: the 1581 count is under 25 k and the 1675–99 figure is 22.3 k, so about 25 k c.1600.
  - Caribbean zone: falls from 200 k (1524) to 38.8 k (1675–99), so about 70–80 k c.1600.

**Where the hemispheric total sits.** The ~11.4 M sum falls between the Koch/2019 figure of about 6 M (very low, model-based) and the sum of high-count regional reconstructions (15–19 M). The Latin American part, about 8.6 M, is consistent with Wikipedia's "lowest estimate" of about 9 M for 1650. That consistency suggests the 2019 figure is too low for a country-by-country table.

## 2. Mexico: central Mexico after the epidemics, and Mexico by modern state

### Takeaway
Cook & Borah's 1.375 M Indians in central Mexico in 1595 is the standard post-epidemic benchmark. It rests on tribute records and is far less contested than their 25 M contact figure. Whole-country estimates for 1595 run from 1.1 M (Zambardino) to 3.5 M (Rosenblat). A defensible total for present-day Mexico c.1595 is about 2.3 M including non-Indians, broken down as:
- central Mexico ≈ 1.375 M;
- Yucatán about 0.17 M;
- Chiapas about 0.1 M;
- the north (Gran Chichimeca, Nueva Vizcaya, Sinaloa–Sonora) about 0.45 M;
- non-Indians about 0.2 M.

**Defensibility.** Cook & Borah's 1595 central-Mexico figure, plus peripheral regions and non-Indians, gives about 2.1–2.5 M. That matches Mendizábal (2.4 M), Aguirre-Beltrán (2.0 M) and the 2.5 M figure on geo-mexico. Zambardino's 1.1–1.7 M is a defensible low case. Rosenblat's 3.5 M is an outlier.

**Trend after 1590.** The game should treat 1590 as near, but not at, the nadir. The decline continued to the early or mid 17th century: McCaa places the nadir in the 17th century, and the 1576–80 *cocoliztli* aftershocks and the 1590s epidemics were still recent.

## 3. Peru: N. D. Cook's *Demographic Collapse* (Toledo census and later visitas) by region

## 4. Brazil c.1600: Portuguese coast with enslaved Africans vs Tupi and interior peoples

### Takeaway
The colonial sphere was small: about 60 k in 1585 (Anchieta), probably 75–100 k by 1600, rising to at least 80 k by c.1612. Of these, Portuguese were roughly 20–30 k, enslaved Africans about 13–25 k, and Christian or *aldeia* Indians about 17–30 k. Pernambuco and Bahia held most of them.

Contreiras Rodrigues's widely copied 100 k for 1600 (30 k whites, 70 k others) rests on a "extremely dubious" source. The indigenous population outside colonial control is the dominant and least-known component: plausibly 0.7–3 M, best guess about 1.5 M.

### Cited Findings

- **1570 (Gândavo).** 3,440 Portuguese *vizinhos* × 5 = 17,200 people of Portuguese origin.
- **1585 (Anchieta, via Contreiras Rodrigues).** Portuguese *vizinhos* ×5, plus 13,000 African slaves and 17,500 Christian Indians, totalling 57,600. Carrara's own reading of Anchieta is "about 60 thousand inhabitants in 1585".

## 5. USA and Canada c.1600: native peoples by broad culture area (low vs high counts, timing of epidemics)

## 6. Where the main sources disagree, and which figure is most defensible for c.1600

### Takeaway
Disagreement is largest for contact-era totals: 8 M to 112 M for the hemisphere, and 0.9 M to 18 M north of Mexico. It is much smaller for c.1590–1600 wherever Spanish tribute and census records exist (central Mexico, Peru, highland Central America). For those regions the archival series of Cook & Borah, N. D. Cook, Lovell & Lutz and Newson are the most defensible.

The figures most open to challenge are:
- Contreiras Rodrigues's 100 k for Brazil in 1600, built on a dubious source;
- Wikipedia's 45 k for Brazil, which counts colonists only;
- the 2019 hemisphere figure of about 6 M, which looks low against the sum of regional reconstructions.

### Cited Findings
- **Mexico, 1595.**
  - Range: 1.1–1.7 M (Zambardino) to 3.5 M (Rosenblat). Cook & Simpson 2.1–3.0 M; Mendizábal 2.4 M; Aguirre-Beltrán 2.0 M. — [McCaa](https://users.pop.umn.edu/~rmccaa/vircatas/virtab3.htm)
  - Cook & Borah's 1519 range of 18–30 M is the most criticised figure. Their 1595 figure of 1.375 M is tribute-based, but they themselves warn that their samples vary. — [UC Press](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/essays-in-population-history-vol-iii-mexico-and-california.html)
  - David Henige (1998) said many estimates rest on "arbitrary formulas". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_history_of_the_Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas)
- **Peru.** Contact estimates range from 3.3 M (Cook's low) to about 9 M (Cook's high), with 3–4 M as the convergent minimum. Cook's post-1570 series is anchored on the Toledo census. — [BCRP Compendio](https://estadisticas.bcrp.gob.pe/estadisticas/series/documentos/Anexo-CHE-Tomo-II-597.pdf)

### Inferences
**Most defensible figures for the game (c.1590):**
- **Central Mexico.** Cook & Borah 1595 (1.375 M), scaled up to about 2.3 M for all of present-day Mexico including non-Indians.
- **Peru.** N. D. Cook 1590 (968 k Indians), plus Amazonia and non-Indians, about 1.17 M.
- **Central America.** Lovell & Lutz / Newson / Gerhard series interpolated: Guatemala about 0.27 M; the isthmus from Guatemala to Panama about 0.63 M.
- **Brazil.** Carrara's colonial reading (about 75–80 k), plus a separately modelled free native population of about 1.5 M.
- **North of Mexico.** Mid-range contact estimates (Snow, Milner, Denevan-92), with limited pre-1600 decline, about 2.75 M.

**Caribbean.** The 2020 genetic finding implies that high Caribbean contact estimates (Denevan's 3 M) are irrelevant by 1590 anyway. The islands were essentially repopulated by Europeans and Africans, a few tens of thousands in all, with Hispaniola's 1606 census as the anchor.

A low run would use Zambardino's figure for Mexico, the Rosenblat-type contact baselines for Brazil, Canada and the US, and the Koch-like total.
