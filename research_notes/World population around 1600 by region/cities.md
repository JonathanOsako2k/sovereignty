# Largest cities of the world c.1590–1600: populations and coordinates

## Conventions

**Population columns**
- **Best (k):** the value I recommend for a game starting on 1 January 1590, in thousands of people. A `*` means the value is my judgement among the cited figures, not a published number.
- **Range (k):** the spread of all figures found for that city at or near 1600.
- **Year:** the date the figure refers to. "c.1500", "c.1550" and "c.1575" are column dates in Chandler-style tables.

**Confidence (Conf.)**
- **H:** a contemporary count or census, or at least two standard compilations agreeing within about 15%.
- **M:** one standard compilation (Chandler, de Vries or "Etext") with no strong contradiction, or sources agreeing within about 30%.
- **L:** sources differ more than 2×; or the column alignment is uncertain; or the figure is for the wrong date; or it rests on chronicles or travellers.
- **U:** no population figure was found. Only an unverified size tier is given.

**Coordinates.** I did NOT fetch coordinates from a gazetteer. I supplied them from general geographic knowledge of each city's historic centre, to about ±0.05°. Rows marked "±0.2" are rougher. Check them against GeoNames or Wikipedia infoboxes before shipping.

**Source abbreviations**
- **HUCS:** Wikipedia, "Historical urban community sizes" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_urban_community_sizes).
  - Cells cite footnote [59] "Chandler" (Chandler 1987, or Chandler & Fox 1974), footnote [70] "Etext" (an online reproduction of city-size data), de Vries (1984), Ruiz and others.
  - The page text is flattened. Where a row has blank cells, I reconstructed which value belongs to which year; those rows are marked "alignment uncertain".
- **LECH:** Wikipedia, "List of largest European cities in history" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_European_cities_in_history).
  - Its Chandler (1987) table has columns …1500, 1550, 1575, 1600, 1650. I aligned each row from its right-hand end.
  - This alignment reproduces independent checks: Paris 185k in 1500 and 245k in 1600 (IROWS), and London 187k in 1600 (Civixplorer).
- **LLCH:** Wikipedia, "List of largest cities throughout history" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_cities_throughout_history).
- **AHE:** African History Extra, "Africa's urban past and economy" (https://www.africanhistoryextra.com/p/africas-urban-past-and-economy-currencies). It cites Chandler, Hunwick, Saad and Kusimba.

## Key question 1: the world's ~150 largest cities c.1600, with coordinates, best estimate, range, year and source

### Takeaway
I found citable population figures for 100 cities. Almost all trace back to Chandler (1987), de Vries (1984) or Chandler's "Etext" series, as reproduced on Wikipedia; a few come from chronicle- or census-based studies. Another 56 candidate cities, listed in Inferences, have coordinates and an unverified size tier only, which brings the list to 156.

The ranking at the top is robust:
- **About 700k:** Beijing and Istanbul.
- **200–500k:** Agra, Lahore, Ahmedabad, Nanjing, Kyoto, Hangzhou, Cairo, Paris, Naples, Osaka and London.

Below about 150k, the figures thin out quickly. Most of sub-Saharan Africa, Southeast Asia, the Deccan, Persia and the secondary Chinese cities have no reliable c.1600 figure on the open web.

### Cited Findings

**Headline cross-check.** All three standard compilations make Beijing the largest city in 1600: Chandler (1987) gives 706,000, Morris (2010) 700,000 and Modelski (2003) 1,000,000 ([LLCH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_cities_throughout_history)).

**Popular derivative, not scholarly.** Ollie Bye's spreadsheet, shown as a JetPunk quiz, gives this 1600 order: Beijing 700k, Constantinople 700k, Lahore 430k, Agra 370k, Kyoto 350k, Hangzhou 270k, Vijayanagara 240k. Osaka enters his top 7 only in 1620, at 300k ([JetPunk](https://www.jetpunk.com/user-quizzes/1428462/biggest-cities-by-decade-1600-1690)). This is useful only as a guide to what players will "expect".

**Method warnings**
- **Ottoman household multipliers.** Ottoman city figures come from tahrir household registers. The multiplier per household ranges from 3 to 5, which shifts totals by about ±40%. Barkan's coefficient of 5 was long the standard ([Bilecik summary](https://bilecik.academia.edu/ertcam)).
- **Arab cities.** Raymond infers growth in Ottoman-era Arab cities largely from their built-up area roughly doubling ([review of Raymond 1985](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/275483996_Andre_Raymond_Grandes_villes_arabes_a_l'epoque_ottomane_La_bibliotheque_arabe_Paris_Sindbad_1985_Pp_389)). His census-based Aleppo study (IJMES 16, 1984) exists, but its figures were not visible to me. The abstract calls earlier chronicle and traveller estimates "fanciful and divergent" ([Cambridge abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-journal-of-middle-east-studies/article/abs/population-of-aleppo-in-the-sixteenth-and-seventeenth-centuries-according-to-ottoman-census-documents/68799E7D8EF1EC49C19CD7887372FD21)).
- **Which compilation to prefer.** The IROWS urban-size studies rank Morris first, then Modelski, then Chandler, where they disagree ([IROWS](https://irows.ucr.edu/papers/irows98/irows98.htm)).

**B. Adjustments for a 1 January 1590 start.**

These are my inferences from general historical knowledge, except where a link is given.
- **Edo:** Ieyasu entered Edo only in 1590. Start it small (about 10–20k) and let it grow toward 60k by 1600.
- **Osaka:** booming as Hideyoshi's castle city. Start near the low end, about 200k.
- **Isfahan:** not yet the capital (that came in 1598). Start it near 80–125k and treat Qazvin as the B/A capital.
- **Tabriz:** under Ottoman occupation 1585–1603, so set it below its 16th-century peak (about 100–150k).
- **Golconda and Hyderabad:** Hyderabad was founded in 1591–92 ([Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/hyderabad)), so in 1590 the 80k sits at Golconda.
- **Lahore and Fatehpur Sikri:** Fatehpur Sikri was abandoned c.1585, and Lahore was the Mughal court city in 1590. Lahore at 350k or more is justified at game start.
- **Antwerp and Amsterdam:** Antwerp fell in 1585 and should start near 50k. Amsterdam should start below 48k and grow quickly.
- **Pegu:** still the Toungoo capital in 1590 (about 150k). It was destroyed in 1599.
- **Gao:** at its peak, about 76–100k. It falls after the Moroccan conquest of 1591.
- **Seoul:** intact in 1590; devastated 1592–98.
- **Ayutthaya:** recovering from the 1569 sack, when it fell to 10k ([HUCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_urban_community_sizes)). About 60–100k suits 1590.
- **Vijayanagara and Gaur:** ruins or small towns. Ignore Ollie Bye's 240k for Vijayanagara in 1600.
- **Madrid:** Chandler's 80k suits 1590, when the court was resident. De Vries's 49k probably reflects the court's move to Valladolid in 1601–06; Valladolid should be boosted in that window.
- **Potosí:** about 110–130k in 1590, interpolating between about 40k (1571) and about 150k (1600).
- **Lima:** about 15–20k in 1590, extrapolated back from 26k in 1614.
- **Manila:** about 10–20k in 1590, rising to about 30k by 1600–03.

**C. Single choices where the sources split.**
- **Naples:** de Vries's figure, often quoted at about 280k, could not be verified here. Use Chandler's 224k and do not use the 400k in HUCS.
- **Cairo:** 250k, between Chandler's 400k for 1500 and 200k for 1600.
- **Nanjing:** 450k, Cao's modern figure. Chandler's 194k looks too low for the Ming southern capital, and the 1M rests on memoir testimony.
- **Bijapur:** about 300k for 1590, shading the 500k–1M early-17th-century peak (via Eaton) for date and for Dyson's critique.

## Key question 2: which cities' figures are most uncertain, and which figure is most defensible

### Takeaway
The widest spreads are:
- **Beijing:** 600k–1M.
- **Agra:** 370k–700k, and a modern demographer argues Mughal cities are overstated.
- **Nanjing:** 194k–1M.
- **Istanbul:** 400–700k.
- **Cairo:** 200–400k.
- **Isfahan:** 125–500k.
- **Bijapur:** 500k–1M claimed.
- **Dhaka:** Bowrey's 900k in 1669.
- **Mataram:** 10k–800k.
- **Potosí:** about 107–160k; "200k+" is unsourced.

The most defensible choices are:
- Beijing about 700k (Morris and Chandler agree).
- Istanbul about 700k, the conventional figure.
- Potosí 120–150k around 1600.
- Agra shaded below Chandler's 500k, to about 400k.

### Cited Findings
- **Beijing, 1600.** Chandler 706k, Morris 700k, Modelski 1M ([LLCH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_cities_throughout_history)). The IROWS studies prefer Morris, then Modelski, then Chandler ([IROWS](https://irows.ucr.edu/papers/irows98/irows98.htm)).
- **Istanbul, 1600.** Chandler 700k ([LECH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_European_cities_in_history)); HUCS gives a range of 400–700k ([HUCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_urban_community_sizes)). The household multiplier (3–5) moves Ottoman totals by about ±40% ([Bilecik](https://bilecik.academia.edu/ertcam)).
- **Agra.**
  - Chandler 500k (1600), 660k (1650) ([HUCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_urban_community_sizes)).
  - Ollie Bye 370k ([JetPunk](https://www.jetpunk.com/user-quizzes/1428462/biggest-cities-by-decade-1600-1690)).
  - 500–700k, uncited ([banotes](https://banotes.org/india-c-1206-1707/medieval-india-urban-centers-demographics-implications)).
  - Dyson (OUP) says previous research "appears to have overstated the size of Mughal cities" ([OUP](https://academic.oup.com/book/3581/chapter/144861931), via search summary).
- **Lahore.** Chandler 350k vs Ollie Bye 430k ([HUCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_urban_community_sizes); [JetPunk](https://www.jetpunk.com/user-quizzes/1428462/biggest-cities-by-decade-1600-1690)).
- **Bijapur.** "Half a million to a million" at its early-17th-century peak, citing Eaton ([Wikipedia: Bijapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijapur), via search summary).
- **Dhaka.** Bowrey (1669–70) gives about 900k, "more populous than London" ([Wikipedia: Dhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaka), via search summary).
- **Nanjing.**
  - Etext 194k ([HUCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_urban_community_sizes)).
  - Cao 450k, with Xu et al. revising toward about 1M ([KNAW](https://pure.knaw.nl/ws/files/8633887/Urbanization_in_China_ca._1100_C1900.pdf), via search summary).
  - The same paper calls the basis of Rozman's 6–7% Ming urbanization rate "rather unclear".

- **Osaka.** 200–360k for 1600 ([HUCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_urban_community_sizes)).
- **Isfahan.** Chandler about 125k vs travellers' 200k (1627–29) and 500k (1637). Iranica says its exact population "remains unknown" ([Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/isfahan-iii-population)).
- **Potosí.**
  - "At least 150,000" by 1600 ([Commonplace](https://commonplace.online/article/high-place-potosi/)).
  - The 1603 description implies at least 131k ([Wikipedia: Potosí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potos%C3%AD)). The same article's "exceeded 200,000" is unsourced, and its "second largest after London" claim is tagged dubious.

- **Gao.** Etext 60k vs a 76k house count vs about 100k in the Tarikh al-Fattash ([HUCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_urban_community_sizes); [AHE](https://www.africanhistoryextra.com/p/africas-urban-past-and-economy-currencies)).

### Inferences
- **Recommended values:**
  - Beijing 700k.
  - Istanbul 700k; 550–600k if a conservative household multiplier is preferred.
  - Agra about 400k; treat 750k-type figures as travellers' impressions.
  - Lahore about 350k in 1590.
  - Nanjing about 450k.
  - Cairo about 250k.
  - Potosí about 120k in 1590, rising to about 150k by 1600–10.
  - Isfahan about 100k in 1590, growing to 300–500k by the 1630s–70s.
  - Bijapur about 300k.
  - Mataram about 50k.
  - Dhaka small in 1590.
- **Europe is reliable; the rest is not.** De Vries and Chandler agree within about 15% for Paris, London, Milan, Rome, Lisbon and Seville, so the two can be treated as interchangeable in a game. Madrid (49k vs 80k) is the only material European disagreement, and it is explained by the court's movements.
- **Reliability by region, most to least reliable:**
  1. Western and Southern Europe (counts: Venice 1581, Seville 1597, Bologna 1588).
  2. The Ottoman core and Japan.
  3. China (disputes over whether walled-city or suburb boundaries are counted).
  4. Mughal and Deccan India (travellers' comparisons with London or Paris).
  5. Southeast Asia and sub-Saharan Africa (chronicles, house and school counts, extrapolations from military strength).

- No modern critical estimates were found for Agra, Lahore, Ahmedabad, Bijapur or Patna. The size of Dyson's proposed downward correction is unknown.
- No census-based Istanbul figure for c.1590 (e.g. from İnalcık or Barkan) was found.
- No trustworthy figure was found for Suzhou, Kaifeng or Yangzhou.
- The archival basis of the 150k and 120k figures for Potosí is not named by Commonplace or Wikipedia.
