# East Asia population c.1590–1600 (China, Korea, Japan, Mongolia, Taiwan): totals and sub-national distributions

Conventions used throughout:
* Populations in **thousands (k)**, **present-day borders** unless stated otherwise.
* "1590" = game start (1 Jan 1590). "1600" = the usual scholarly benchmark.
* Confidence grades: **A** = several independent scholarly estimates agree; **B** = one solid scholarly estimate (or a well-sourced range); **C** = scholarly figure seen only via secondary quotation, or a proxy-based allocation (e.g. kokudaka, later registers); **D** = my own inference/interpolation with no direct source; treat as a placeholder.
* Anything not backed by a citation is in an "Inferences" or "Gaps" block and is labelled as such.

## Q1. National totals c.1590/1600 by present-day country / sub-area

### Takeaway
East Asia within present-day borders held roughly 205 million people in 1590: c.175 M in Ming China proper (the plausible range is 150–200 M), c.3 M in the Chinese frontier regions outside Ming registration, c.16–17 M in Japan, c.11 M in Korea before the 1592 invasion, c.0.6 M in Mongolia and c.0.1 M in Taiwan. The China, Japan and Korea totals all sit inside wide scholarly disagreements (±15–30%). Every frontier figure is a weak inference.

### Cited Findings
- **Ming registered population**: 1578 (Wanli 6) 10,621,436 households and 60,692,856 persons; 1602 (Wanli 30) 10,030,241 households and 56,305,050 persons; 1620 51,655,459 — [zh.wikipedia 中国人口史](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E4%B8%AD%E5%9B%BD%E4%BA%BA%E5%8F%A3%E5%8F%B2). The Population Review article likewise gives an official figure of 56.2 M for 1600–1602 — [Population Review 2019](https://populationreview.com/files/716335pp20-77.pdf) (seen via search summary).
- **Scholarly estimates for the late-Ming total**: Ge Jianxiong c.197 M in 1600; Cao Shuji a peak of 192.51 M in 1630 and 152.47 M in 1644; Maddison a peak of c.162 M in 1580–90, then 130 M (1640); Wang Yumin 130–150 M at the Wanli peak; Zhao Wenlin & Xie Shujun c.99.9 M in 1626 — [zh.wikipedia 中国人口史](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E4%B8%AD%E5%9B%BD%E4%BA%BA%E5%8F%A3%E5%8F%B2). Fairbank c.160 M, Brook c.175 M, Ebrey "perhaps as large as 200 M". The article's population table gives a "modern estimate" of 160 M for 1600 and 145 M for 1550 — [en.wikipedia Population history of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_history_of_China).
- **Perkins** gives 120–200 M for 1600. G. William Skinner puts the Ming peak at 200 M in 1585 — [CSDE Working Paper 98-5, Univ. of Washington](https://csde.washington.edu/downloads/98-5.pdf). I saw this only through the search-engine summary because the PDF would not load.
- **Japan 1600**: Hayami 10–14 M (the midpoint, 12 M, "has long been accepted"); Yoshida (1910) 18 M from the rule of 1 koku ≈ 1 person; Maddison 18.5 M; Saito 17 M (unpublished, quoted in Farris 2006 p.262). Saito & Takashima adopt **17 M** for 1600. Their totals exclude Ezo (Hokkaido) and Ryukyu (Okinawa) — [Saito & Takashima 2015, Hitotsubashi RCESR DP15-3](https://risk.ier.hit-u.ac.jp/Japanese/pdf/dp15-3_rcesr.pdf).
- **Japan 1600, other columns**: McEvedy & Jones 22 M; Kito (1996) 12.273 M; Biraben 12 M; Farris (2006) 15–17 M — [en.wikipedia Demographics of Japan before Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Japan_before_Meiji_Restoration).
- **Ryukyu (Okinawa)**: 108,958 people in 1632 (Satsuma survey, not reported to the shogunate). **Ezo**: the earliest combined Matsumae+Ezo figure is 56,461 in 1804, and Ainu counts begin only in 1798–1810 — [en.wikipedia Demographics of Japan before Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Japan_before_Meiji_Restoration).
- **Korea**: Kwon Tai-hwan & Shin Yong-ha (1977) give **14,039k in 1590** and 10,665k in 1639. Han Young-woo gives **9,580k in 1590** and 7,210k in 1519. The registered count was 4,162k in 1543 and 1,521k in 1639. That page's author himself estimates "9–10 million just before the Imjin War" — [우리역사넷, 인구의 증가와 분포](https://contents.history.go.kr/front/nh/view.do?levelId=nh_033_0020_0010_0010). Kwon & Shin's series (Table 4) runs 1500 9.42 M, **1600 11.72 M**, 1650 10.88 M — [동북아역사넷](https://contents.nahf.or.kr/item/level.do?levelId=edeah.d_0004_0030_0020_0030). Tony Michell ("Fact and Hypothesis…", *Korean Studies Forum* 5, 1979–80) puts the mid-16th-century peak at c.10 M, a "ceiling", and estimates that the 1590s war cut the population by c.20% (≈2 M) — [우리역사넷 (Michell summary)](https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/nh/view.do?levelId=nh_030_0050_0030_0010_0030).
- The Wikipedia 1600 list gives Joseon **11,722,000**, attributed to Maddison (the same number as Kwon & Shin's 1600), Japan 12 M (Hayami), "Northern Yuan (Mongols)" ~2.76 M (Chuluun 2014), the Jianzhou Jurchen confederation "<2,000,000" (Crossley, Siu & Sutton 2006, p.30) and Ming 60–150 M — [en.wikipedia List of countries by population in 1600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600).
- **Mongolia**: Wikipedia's 1700 list (from Avakov, mostly Maddison) gives "Outer Mongolia Governorate – 619,000" (seen via search summary) — [en.wikipedia List of countries by population in 1700](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1700). Populstat shows "16xx: 604.0" for Mongolia, but the search summary was garbled and unverified and the page itself would not load — [populstat Mongolia](http://populstat.info/Asia/mongolic.htm).
- **Taiwan**: The Dutch 1650 census counted 64,000–68,000 indigenous people, and "Taiwan's population was probably around 100,000" — [en.wikipedia Dutch Formosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Formosa). In 1622 the Dutch found c.70,000 Austronesian aborigines — [EH.net, Olds, Economic History of Taiwan](https://eh.net/encyclopedia/the-economic-history-of-taiwan/). One source gives 50,000 total in 1650 — [NTNU Population Geography of Taiwan](http://twgeog.ntnugeog.org/en/population_geog/). The 1650 census "covers only a very small swath of Taiwan" (68,000 in 315 villages) — [Asianometry](https://www.asianometry.com/p/taiwans-indigenous-peoples-briefly).
- **Xinjiang**: Wei Yuan put the Dzungars at 600,000 (200,000 households) before their destruction in the 1750s, and modern estimates run 600–750k — [en.wikipedia Dzungar genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzungar_genocide). The Tarim Basin had 320,000 in 1777 and 650,000 in 1826 — [en.wikipedia Timeline of Xinjiang under Qing rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Xinjiang_under_Qing_rule). Altishahr was c.260,000 around 1650 (table seen via search summary) — [en.wikipedia History of Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Xinjiang).
- **Tibet**: A Manchu (Qing) census found c.850,000 lay people plus c.100,000 monks and nuns, while another source of the same period says 3.5 M Tibetans — [Childs, Polyandry and population growth in a historical Tibetan society](https://case.edu/artsci/tibet/sites/default/files/2022-05/Polyandry%20And%20Population%20Growth%20in%20a%20Historical%20Tibetan%20Society.pdf) (via search summary). The 3.5 M "mid-Qing" figure is attributed to two Chinese scholars — [Yan Hao, Tibetan Population in China: Myths and Facts Re-examined](https://www.boell.de/sites/default/files/assets/boell.de/images/download_de/Link_Yan_Hao.pdf) (via search summary).

## Q2. Ming China's total c.1600: registered (~60 M) vs scholarly (150–230 M). Which is most defensible?

### Takeaway
The registered count (60.7 M in 1578, 56.3 M in 1602) measures fiscal households, not people; no modern scholar takes it as a headcount. Real-population estimates cluster in two groups. One is "~150–165 M" (Ho Ping-ti's tradition, Wang Yumin, Maddison, Fairbank). The other is "~190–200 M" (Ge Jianxiong 197 M for 1600, Cao Shuji 192.5 M for 1630, Skinner 200 M for 1585, Ebrey). Cao and Ge's prefecture-by-prefecture reconstruction is the most detailed and recent. For a 1590 start, **~175 M (range 150–200 M)** is the most defensible single figure.

### Cited Findings
- The registered totals stayed roughly flat at 51–62 M after 1393 "while the population was in fact increasing". Brook explains the under-reporting: families under-reported household members, especially daughters, and officials under-reported households — [en.wikipedia Population history of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_history_of_China).
- 1578: 60,692,856 persons; 1602: 56,305,050 "口男妇"; 1620: 51,655,459 — [zh.wikipedia 中国人口史](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E4%B8%AD%E5%9B%BD%E4%BA%BA%E5%8F%A3%E5%8F%B2).
- The 1393 registered total was 60,545,812, which excludes most of North China and is commonly revised to c.65 M — [zh.wikipedia 中国人口史](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E4%B8%AD%E5%9B%BD%E4%BA%BA%E5%8F%A3%E5%8F%B2). Ho Ping-ti revised the 1393 figure to 65 M and Brook to 75 M; Atwell, citing Heijdra and Mote, gives c.90 M around 1400 — [en.wikipedia Population history of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_history_of_China).
- Ge Jianxiong: c.197 M in 1600, a peak "close to 200 million". Cao Shuji: peak 192.51 M in 1630, then 152.47 M in 1644 (a loss of ~40 M from the 1630s–40s plague, drought and war). Maddison: peak 162 M in 1580–90. Wang Yumin: 130–150 M at the Wanli peak. Zhao & Xie: 99.873 M in 1626 — [zh.wikipedia 中国人口史](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E4%B8%AD%E5%9B%BD%E4%BA%BA%E5%8F%A3%E5%8F%B2).
- Perkins gives 120–200 M for 1600 and Skinner 200 M for 1585 — [CSDE WP 98-5](https://csde.washington.edu/downloads/98-5.pdf) (seen via search summary).
- Fairbank ~160 M, Brook ~175 M, Ebrey ~200 M — [en.wikipedia Population history of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_history_of_China). The Ming dynasty article says estimates "vary from 160 to 200 million" — [en.wikipedia Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty).
- Cao Shuji, *Zhongguo renkou shi* vol. 4 (Ming), Fudan UP 2000, reconstructs prefecture-level population for 1393 and province-level population for 1630 — [Baidu Baike entry on vol. 4](https://baike.baidu.com/item/%E4%B8%AD%E5%9B%BD%E4%BA%BA%E5%8F%A3%E5%8F%B2%EF%BC%88%E7%AC%AC%E5%9B%9B%E5%8D%B7%EF%BC%89%E6%98%8E%E6%97%B6%E6%9C%9F/19436868). The 2024 English edition (Cao Shuji, *The Population History of China (1368–1953)*, Brill, Global Economic History Series 20) gives **prefectural estimates for 1393, 1580, 1630**, 1680, 1776, 1850, 1880, 1910 and 1953 — [Quantitative History book launch page](https://quantitativehistory.org/events/Quantitative-History-Book-Launch-Series-The-Population-History-of-China-1368%E2%80%931953) (via search summary). The **1580 benchmark is ideal for a 1590 start**, but I could not read its numbers.
- A quoted reading of Cao's vol. 4 (p.235) gives an average growth rate of 0.41%/yr from 1391 to 1630 (via search summary).
- Cao's 1997 plague article estimated North China in 1580 (Wanli 8) as: Beizhili + Shanxi ≈ 17.8 M (1582) and Henan ≈ 7.8 M (c.1580), 25.6 M in total, falling to ~12.8 M by c.1644 — [Cao Shuji, 鼠疫流行与华北社会的变迁 (1580—1644年), via 爱思想](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/120213.html). His 2000 book puts Henan in 1630 at 16.73 M (see Q3), so his provincial figures were revised sharply upward between 1997 and 2000.
- Heijdra (Cambridge History of China vol. 8): I found **no accessible statement of his c.1600 figure**. The "~231 M" upper bound in the brief could not be verified. Atwell's citation of Heijdra for ~90 M around 1400 is the only Heijdra number I found — [en.wikipedia Population history of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_history_of_China).
- A 2025 study by Julia C. Schneider compares the *Mingshilu*, *Huidian* and gazetteer figures. It warns that post-1393 data show some southern regions with negative or minimal growth, so uniform-growth assumptions should not be applied — [Schneider, "Crunching Numbers in Ming Times", JEACS 6(2) 2025](https://journals.univie.ac.at/index.php/jeacs/article/view/10068).

### Inferences
- **Recommendation: 175 M at 1 Jan 1590 within Ming territory (range 150–200 M). Grade B.** Reasoning:
  1. The Fudan school (Ge and Cao), the only full prefecture-level reconstruction, puts c.1600–1630 at 192–197 M.
  2. Back-projecting Cao's 192.5 M (1630) at 0.25–0.4%/yr gives ~170–180 M for 1590–1600.
  3. Western syntheses (Maddison 162 M, Fairbank 160 M, Brook 175 M) sit just below.
  4. 175 M is therefore the overlap point. A designer who wants to follow Cao and Ge strictly can use 185–190 M; one who follows Maddison can use 160 M. All provincial shares in Q3 scale linearly.
- The registered 60 M should not be used as population. It is useful only as a "taxable registered population" for the game's fiscal model: about 1/3 of the real population.
- Ho Ping-ti's ~150 M for 1600 (stated in older literature; I did not verify it directly here) is now regarded as the low end.

### Gaps
- Heijdra's CHC vol. 8 figure and Ho Ping-ti's 1600 number were not verified from accessible pages.
- I could not access the Cao 2024 (Brill) 1580 prefectural table, which would be the best single source for this game. It is worth obtaining from a library copy, not a pirate site.

## Q3. China by modern province c.1590 (ISO 3166-2 CN-xx)

### Takeaway
Cao Shuji's 1630 provincial estimates are the best available distribution. I found 13 of the 18 provincial units only as quotations in secondary (mostly critical) Chinese articles. I filled the remaining five (Nan Zhili, Shandong, Shanxi, Shaanxi proper, Liaodong) as the residual against Cao's 192.5 M total, then scaled everything to 175 M for 1590 and split Ming provinces into modern ones. The top four provinces by size are Zhejiang, Jiangxi, Shandong and Jiangsu (Nan Zhili). The frontier southwest (Yunnan and Guizhou) has about 1.2–1.3% each.

## Q5. Korea c.1590 (Joseon) by province, mapped to KR-/KP- codes

### Takeaway
Pre-war Joseon held **~11 M (range 9.5–14 M)**, compared with a registered count of only ~4 M (1543). Provincial registers survive in usable form only after the war (1648 onward). The first fairly complete late-17th-century registers (1669–1678) and the 1789 Hogu chongsu give the shape: the three southern provinces (Gyeongsang, Jeolla, Chungcheong) held ~50–53%, the three northern provinces (Hwanghae, Pyeongan, Hamgyeong) held ~28–32%, and Gyeonggi + Gangwon + Hanseong held ~17–19%. Before 1592 the south's share was probably somewhat higher, because the north filled up through 17th–18th-century settlement. Roughly two-thirds of Joseon's population lived in what is now South Korea.

## Q6. Where sources disagree, and which figures are most defensible

### Takeaway
The biggest disagreements are over Ming China's level (60 M registered vs 150–200 M real, and within the real estimates ~160 M vs ~195 M); over Japan (12 M Hayami/Kito vs 17–18.5 M Saito/Farris/Maddison, and an outlier 22 M from McEvedy & Jones); and over Korea (9.6 M Han Young-woo vs 14.0 M Kwon-Shin). The frontier regions have almost no scholarship.

### Cited Findings
- **China**: 56–61 M registered (1578–1602) vs Ge 197 M (1600), Cao 192.5 M (1630), Maddison 162 M (1580–90), Wang 130–150 M, Zhao 99.9 M (1626) — [zh.wikipedia 中国人口史](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E4%B8%AD%E5%9B%BD%E4%BA%BA%E5%8F%A3%E5%8F%B2). Perkins gives 120–200 M — [CSDE 98-5](https://csde.washington.edu/downloads/98-5.pdf). The Population Review article questions multiplying 56.2 M up to 150 M — [Population Review 2019](https://populationreview.com/files/716335pp20-77.pdf). The timing of the peak is disputed: Maddison 1580–90, Ge c.1600, Cao 1630 — [zh.wikipedia 中国人口史](https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-cn/%E4%B8%AD%E5%9B%BD%E4%BA%BA%E5%8F%A3%E5%8F%B2).
- **Japan**: "There are two competing estimates for 1600": Hayami's 10–14 M and Yoshida's 18 M. Saito & Takashima "believe … not very different from the Yoshida-Maddison figure" and adopt 17 M — [Saito & Takashima 2015](https://risk.ier.hit-u.ac.jp/Japanese/pdf/dp15-3_rcesr.pdf). McEvedy & Jones's 22 M is an outlier — [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Japan_before_Meiji_Restoration).
- **Korea**: 1590 estimates of 14,039k (Kwon-Shin) vs 9,580k (Han Young-woo); 9–10 M per the 우리역사넷 author — [우리역사넷](https://contents.history.go.kr/front/nh/view.do?levelId=nh_033_0020_0010_0010). Michell's ~10 M peak — [우리역사넷 (Michell)](https://contents.history.go.kr/mobile/nh/view.do?levelId=nh_030_0050_0030_0010_0030).
- **Taiwan**: 50k (NTNU) vs 64–68k (Dutch 1650 census) vs ~100k whole island — sources as in Q1.
- **Tibet**: 0.95 M (Qing count incl. clergy) vs 3.5 M ("mid-Qing", whole plateau) — sources as in Q1.

- **Consistency for game data**: if the designer prefers a "high" world (Cao/Ge, Saito, Kwon-Shin), multiply China by ~1.08 and Korea by ~1.15. For a "low" world (Maddison, Hayami, Han), use China ×0.92, Japan 12–13 M and Korea ~9.6 M. The provincial shares in Tables 2–6 stay valid under either scaling.
- **Density sanity check** (my calculation, not sourced): Zhejiang at 21.5 M over ~105,000 km² ≈ 200/km², and Japan at 17 M over ~300,000 km² (excluding Hokkaido) ≈ 57/km². Korea at 11 M over ~220,000 km² ≈ 50/km², which matches the 동북아역사넷 remark that pre-war Korean density was about ten times the c.1600 Southeast Asian average of 5.8/km² — [동북아역사넷](https://contents.nahf.or.kr/item/level.do?levelId=edeah.d_0004_0030_0020_0030).
