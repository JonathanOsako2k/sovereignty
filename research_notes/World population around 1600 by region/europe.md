# Population of Europe (incl. European Russia and Siberia) c.1590–1600, by present-day country and first-level region

Conventions used throughout these notes:

- Figures are in **thousands** and refer to **present-day borders** unless stated.
- "Best c.1600" is my synthesis of the cited figures. "1 Jan 1590 value" is my suggested value for the game start, interpolated from the c.1600 figure using whatever trend evidence exists.
- Confidence codes:
  - **A**: national historical demography (census, fiscal count or reconstruction).
  - **B**: reputable compilation (Maddison or Avakov via Wikipedia, OWID, De Vries-type lists).
  - **C**: my inference from partial anchors.
  - **D**: a modelled placeholder with no numeric source found. Use it only until a real figure is found.
- Key compiled sources, abbreviated below:
  - **[A]** = [Wikipedia, "List of countries by population in 1600"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600). It mostly uses Avakov's *Two Thousand Years of Economic Statistics* (2015), which in turn relies mostly on Maddison, with figures "divided into modern borders".
  - **[O]** = [de.wikipedia, "Liste der Länder und Territorien nach Bevölkerungsentwicklung"](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_der_L%C3%A4nder_und_Territorien_nach_Bev%C3%B6lkerungsentwicklung). Its data are taken from Our World in Data; the page does not name the underlying pre-1800 dataset.
  - **[D]** = [1632.org European population compilation](https://1632.org/1632tech/eur_pop/). It names Jan De Vries only for Scandinavia; the other figures are unattributed.

## Q1. National totals c.1600 (present-day borders) for every European country, with Russia split into European Russia and Siberia/Far East

### Takeaway
European totals c.1600 can be assembled for every present-day country. Only Italy (Beloch/Malanima), England (Wrigley–Schofield-type reconstruction), Spain (the 1591 Castilian census plus Crown of Aragon fiscal counts) and Malta (the 1590 count) rest on strong national demography. Most of the Balkans, the Baltics, Belarus, Ukraine and the micro-states rest on Avakov/Maddison compilations or my own inference. My country estimates sum to about 91 million for Europe excluding Russia. That fits Biraben's 89 million for Europe without Russia, and OWID's roughly 101.5 million for Europe including Russia's 9.5 million.

**Avakov/Maddison and other figures for 1600 [A]**
- Sweden 760k; Finland 500k; Duchy of Estonia 101k (all Avakov). The page's Sweden total is 1,419k, which does not equal the sum of these sub-entries (1,361k).
- Denmark 650k; Norway 400k; Iceland 50k. The Denmark–Norway total is 1,361k, which also does not match its sub-entries.
- England 4,150k; Wales 250k; Ireland 1,200k; Channel Islands 20k and Isle of Man 5k ("Jersey's History"; Dury 1948). The Kingdom of Scotland is given as 1,180k (Tyson).
- Spanish Netherlands 1,600k+; a note says this counts only present-day Belgium. Dutch Republic 1,500k (Avakov). Swiss Republic 1,000k.
- Habsburg sub-entries:
  - Bohemian Crown 2,950k (Wilson).
  - "Archduchy of Austria and related territories" 2,500k in one place and 2,100k in the Habsburg sub-list.
  - Kingdom of Hungary (Royal Hungary) 1,800k.
  - Kingdom of Croatia about 647k.
  - Duchy of Carniola: 248k in the Holy Roman Empire list and 290k in the Habsburg list.
- Ottoman Europe: Greece 1,500k; Bulgaria 1,250k; Serbia 1,057k; Bosnia Eyalet 449k; Ottoman Hungary 900k; Ottoman Albania 200k (Maddison 2001).
- Ottoman vassals: Transylvania 965k (Keul 2009); Moldavia 440k and Wallachia 300k (Murgescu 2016); Crimean Khanate 187k (Davies 2014).
- Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth about 11,722k (Wilson).
- Tsardom of Russia about 10,000k. Avakov gives 10.1 million for European Russia; Gorskaya (1994) gives 7–10 million.
- Kingdom of Spain 8,240k (Avakov). Portugal 1,100k (Wilson). Kingdom of France 22,000k (Wilson p.152). Republic of Ragusa 60k (Vekarić 1998).
- All of the above is from [A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600). The page itself warns that recalculating to modern borders makes the figures "likely inaccurate for the period".

**De Vries-type list [D]**, c.1600 unless noted
- Scandinavia about 2 million (De Vries).
- Denmark–Norway about 1.2 million and Sweden–Finland about 900k, both "early 17th c." (Peter Englund).
- England 4.2m; Scotland 1m; Ireland 1m.
- Northern Netherlands 1.5m; Spanish Netherlands 1.5m.

- Austria–Bohemia 4.3m; Poland 3.4m; Switzerland 0.9m; Italian peninsula 13.1m.
- Source: [1632.org](https://1632.org/1632tech/eur_pop/)

**Italy**
- Italy within Beloch's frame (310,000 km², including Corsica, Malta, Nice, Monaco and Istria): 11,500k in 1550, 13,273k in 1600 and 11,500k in 1650.
- Centre-North was 7,828k and South-and-Islands 5,445k in 1600. Centre-North was 7,200k in 1580 and 7,500k in 1590.
- Malanima says the margins of uncertainty for Italy's late-medieval and early-modern population are smaller than for other European countries.
- Source: [Malanima, Tab. A.3–A.4 and p.5](https://www.mulino.it/edizioni/volumi/espansioni/malanima/appendice_malanima.pdf)
- Bellettini's estimate for Italy in 1600 is 13.5 million. — [Univ. Trieste course PDF](https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/156837/mod_resource/content/2/Stagnazione%20popolazione%20italiana%20300-600.pdf)

**Spain**
- Ruiz Martín estimated **6,617,251** people for the Crown of Castile from the 1591 census.
  - Ruiz Martín's earlier total for all Spain was 8.1 million. Nadal lowered it to 6.6 million by using a multiplier of 4 people per *vecino* (household).
  - Multipliers in use range from below 4 to 5; 5 is the most common.
  - Late-16th-century estimates for Spain span 6.6 to 9.9 million.
  - Portugal had 1.0–1.2 million in the 16th and early 17th centuries.
  - There were about 325,000 moriscos at the start of the 17th century. Their 1609 expulsion removed 16.2% of the Crown of Aragon's population.
  - Source: [es.wikipedia, "Evolución de la población española en la época precensal"](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evoluci%C3%B3n_de_la_poblaci%C3%B3n_espa%C3%B1ola_en_la_%C3%A9poca_precensal)
- Lanza García gives the Crown of Castile 5,302,000 people in 1591 and 7,298,000 in 1787. — [Modernalia](https://www.modernalia.es/items/show/1358)
- Ladero Quesada writes that the population of the peninsula rose through the 16th century to about **8,100,000 around 1590, not including Portugal**.
  - Annual growth from 1530 to 1591 was estimated at 7.66‰ in Castile, 5.91‰ in Valencia and about 3.2‰ in Aragon and Catalonia.
  - Source: [Ladero Quesada, "Población de las ciudades en la baja Edad Media", p.181](https://chi.guimaraes.pt/actas/1CHI/vol1/1chi-vol1-007.pdf)
- Sources disagree on what the 1591 census covered:
  - One source says it covered all of the Crown of Castile except the three Basque provinces and Navarre.
  - The Biblioteca Nacional de España says it excluded the Kingdom of Granada and the Basque provinces.
  - It counted *vecinos* (households), mainly tax-paying ones.
  - Sources: [es.wikipedia, "Libro de los millones"](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libro_de_los_millones); [BNE](https://bne.es/es/preguntas-frecuentes/censos-catastros-siglo-xvi-puedo-consultar); [INE edition](https://www.ine.es/ss/Satellite?L=es_ES&c=INEPublicacion_C&cid=1259949890789&p=1254735110672&pagename=ProductosYServicios/PYSLayout&param1=PYSDetalleGratuitas)

**England and Wales**
- A generalised inverse-projection table gives England (with Wales) about 4.22 million in 1591 and about 4.46 million in 1601. England alone was about 3.94 million in 1591 and about 4.16 million in 1601. — [J. Munro, Univ. Toronto population tables](https://www.economics.utoronto.ca/munro5/14POPERMTables.doc)
- The reconstruction lineage behind such tables is Wrigley & Schofield's *Population History of England 1541–1871* (1981/1989). — [catalogue record](https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/000226515)

**France**
- Population within present-day French borders: 18.5 million in 1600 and 21 million in 1650 (citing Haine 2000). — [Seshat](https://seshat-db.com/core/polity/460)
- France's population in 1600 "exceeded 19.0 million". — [RUDN article](https://repository.rudn.ru/en/records/article/record/68755)
- A Wikipedia-derived table for metropolitan France gives 20m (1580), 18.5m (1594) and 20m (1600). It carries a "citation needed" tag. — [mirror of "Demographics of France"](https://wikipedia2006.classicistranieri.com/en/d/e/m/Demographics_of_France_f210.html)
- The tacitus.nu table gives France 20.0m in 1600, Spain 8.0m and Portugal 2.0m. No sources are given. — [tacitus.nu](https://tacitus.nu/historical-atlas/population/westeurope.htm)

**Germany**
- The territory of the Holy Roman Empire had about 15 million people around 1600 (Kötzschke), against about 12 million around 1500. — [bpb, "Der demografische Wandel in Deutschland"](https://www.bpb.de/shop/zeitschriften/izpb/demografischer-wandel-350/507785/der-demografische-wandel-in-deutschland/)
- Germany had 16–17 million people at the start of the Thirty Years' War and about 10 million in 1648. — [bpb, "Deutschlands großer Konfessionskrieg"](https://www.bpb.de/themen/zeit-kulturgeschichte/reformation/235721/deutschlands-grosser-konfessionskrieg/) (as summarised in search results)
- Pfister and Fertig use Christian Pfister's estimate for 1600, adjusted to their territory, as an anchor. The figure itself was not obtained. — [Pfister/Fertig, academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/1616736/The_population_history_of_Germany_research_strategy_and_preliminary_results)

**Russia and Siberia**
- Gorskaya (1994) gives the Grand Duchy of Moscow about 5.8 million in 1500, growing to 9–10 million by 1550.
- Vodarsky, as reported by Gorskaya, puts the mid-16th-century population at 6.5 million, rising to 7 million by the end of the century.
- The 1678 census counted 950,000 households, implying 10.5–11.5 million people.
- After the Time of Troubles, the population only regained its 1600 level fifty years later.
- The Novgorod land had 500–800 thousand people in the late 15th century.
- The Grand Duchy of Lithuania had about 3.6 million people around 1500.
- Sources for all of the above: [Wikipedia, "Demographic history of Russia"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_history_of_Russia)
- Parker's *Historical Geography of Russia* (1968) gives about 12,000,000 for 1600. — [Univ. Oregon demographic table](https://pages.uoregon.edu/kimball/nsx.htm)
- "According to some estimates", Siberia's indigenous peoples numbered 160,000 by the beginning of the 17th century. The estimator is not named, and I took this from a search snippet without verifying it on the page. — [Wikipedia, "Indigenous peoples of Siberia"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_Siberia)

**Poland–Lithuania**
- The Commonwealth had about 7.5 million people on about 865,000 km² in 1580. — [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Polish-Lithuanian_Commonwealth); [Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q172107)
- It had about 8 million people on about 800,000 km² in the period of the Union of Lublin. — [ZPE (Polish Ministry of Education)](https://zpe.gov.pl/a/przeczytaj/D5MBG6MZX)
- Around 1569 there were roughly 4.5 million Poles, 0.75 million Lithuanians, 0.7 million Jews and 2 million Ruthenians. — [New World Encyclopedia](https://www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Polish-Lithuanian_Commonwealth)

**Smaller countries**
- Malta: the 1590 count by the Order of St John gave 28,864 people, of whom 1,864 lived on Gozo. The population fell to 21,102 by 1595 after famine, plague and departures. — [Geneanum, "The population of Malta in the seventeenth century"](https://en.geneanum.com/malta/documents/history/the-population-of-malta-in-the-seventeenth-century-a-reflection-of-modernity.html)
- Iceland's population is believed to have stayed between 40,000 and 60,000 from the 10th to the mid-19th century. — [tacitus.nu Scandinavia](https://tacitus.nu/historical-atlas/population/scandinavia.htm)

## Q2. Sub-national distributions c.1600 (modern first-level divisions)

### Takeaway
Only Italy and Spain have regional figures anchored in historical demography that I could access:
- Italy: Beloch/Malanima macro-areas and densities, Tuscany directly, plus figures for the old states.
- Spain: Ruiz Martín's regional split of Castile c.1530, Ladero's figures for the Crown of Aragon, Navarre and the Canaries, and growth rates for 1530–91.

For the British Isles I have nation-level figures but not English regional ones; Wrigley's 2009 county estimates and Rickman's 1600 county table exist but were not accessible. For France, Germany, Poland, Ukraine, European Russia, Siberia and Portugal, I found only scattered anchors. The tables for those countries are explicitly modelled placeholders (D) and should be replaced when primary tables are found.

### Cited Findings

**Italy**
- In 1600, by Beloch's macro-areas:
  - Densities: North 50.9/km², Centre 41.8, South 42.4, Islands 27.5; Italy overall 42.9.
  - Malanima's areas: North = Piedmont (with Aosta, Nice and Monaco), Lombardy, Veneto, Trentino-Alto Adige, Friuli, Istria, Liguria and Emilia-Romagna. Centre = Tuscany, Umbria and Marche. South = Lazio, Abruzzo, Molise, Campania, Puglia, Basilicata and Calabria. Islands = Sicily, Sardinia, Corsica and the minor islands, including Malta.

## Q3. Where the main sources disagree, and which figure is most defensible for c.1590–1600

### Takeaway
The biggest disagreements are:
- **Spain:** 6.6 vs 8.1–8.5m, driven by the household multiplier (4 vs 5).
- **Russia:** 7m (Vodarsky) vs 9.5–12m (OWID, Avakov, Parker).
- **Poland–Lithuania:** 7.5–8m in 1580 vs 11.7m in 1600 (Wilson).
- **France:** 18.3–22m. The 22m (Wilson) is an outlier; 18.5–20m is the defensible band.
- **Germany:** 12m vs 15–17m. This is mostly a matter of territory, not substance.
- **Finland:** 0.5m (Avakov) vs about 0.3m implied by Englund.

Italy (about 13.0–13.3m) and the Netherlands (1.5m) are near-consensus. Where national demography exists (Malanima for Italy, Wrigley–Schofield-type reconstruction for England, the 1591 census for Castile, the Malta count), it is more defensible than Maddison or Avakov.

**Spain**
- Ruiz Martín's earlier figure for all of Spain was 8.1 million; Nadal reduced it to 6.6 million by using a multiplier of 4. Multipliers range from below 4 to 5, with 5 the most common. — [es.wikipedia (precensal)](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evoluci%C3%B3n_de_la_poblaci%C3%B3n_espa%C3%B1ola_en_la_%C3%A9poca_precensal)

**Systematic patterns**
- Maddison/Avakov and OWID-lineage figures are smooth, round-number series, mostly descended from older compilations such as McEvedy & Jones. National reconstructions differ from them by up to about 25% (Spain, Russia, Finland).
- For a game, I would anchor on the national reconstructions (Malanima, the England reconstruction, the Castilian census) and use the compilations only where nothing else exists.
- Year-level adjustments matter for a 1 January 1590 start:
  - Italy: Centre-North 7.50m in 1590 vs 7.83m in 1600.
  - England: 3.94m in 1591 vs 4.16m in 1601.
  - Malta: 28.9k in 1590 vs 21.1k in 1595.
  - Russia: did not regain its 1600 level until about 1650, after the Time of Troubles. The game will need a population crash in 1601–1613, but the 1590 value should not include it.
  - Spain: about 1591 is the late-16th-century peak, before the plague of 1596–1602 and the 1609–14 morisco expulsion.

### Gaps
- I did not consult HYDE 3.x or Clio Infra directly, so their per-country values for 1600 cannot be tabulated here. OWID's pre-1800 source is not stated on the page I used.
- I did not access the primary works of McEvedy & Jones (1978) or Biraben (1979), except Biraben's Europe-without-Russia totals via Malanima. Per-country disagreement with them is therefore not quantified.
- Pfister's 1600 figure for Germany, Dupâquier's for France, Kabuzan's for Russia and Gieysztorowa's 1580 Commonwealth breakdown remain to be checked. Each could shift the recommended figure by 5–15%.
