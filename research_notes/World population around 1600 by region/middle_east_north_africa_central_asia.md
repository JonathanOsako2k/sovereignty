# Population of the Middle East, North Africa, the Caucasus and Central Asia c.1590–1600 (present-day borders)

Scope: national totals for 31 present-day countries/territories, sub-national distributions for the large or uneven ones, and where the sources disagree. All figures are in thousands of people, within present-day borders, unless stated otherwise. Target date: game start, 1 January 1590.

Notes on method:
- The only machine-readable country series I could read online is Avakov's *Two Thousand Years of Economic Statistics*, which mostly follows Maddison. Wikipedia reproduces it for 1600 within modern borders.
- I read McEvedy & Jones (1978) directly from the OCR text of a scanned copy, but that scan **skips the whole Near East chapter (book pp. 133–156)**. That chapter covers Turkey-in-Asia, Syria/Lebanon, Palestine/Jordan, Arabia, Iraq, Iran and Afghanistan. I could therefore cite M&J only for Cyprus, Transcaucasia, Russian Turkestan, the Maghreb, Libya and Egypt.
- HYDE 3.x and Clio Infra country values sit in downloadable datasets. Per the no-download constraint, I did not read them.
- Every "best estimate" and every sub-national share is my own synthesis, and they are labelled as inferences. Treat sub-national shares as modelling assumptions with low confidence unless a cited figure supports them.

## Key question 1: National totals c.1590–1600 (present-day borders)

### Takeaway
The region held roughly 36–40 million people c.1590. The North Africa total of about 9–10 million is my synthesis, not a source total. Avakov's North Africa figure is about 12 million, mostly because he puts Egypt at 5.0m.
- The big countries are Turkey (about 7.3m), Iran (about 5.5m), Egypt (about 3.5m), Morocco (about 2.5m), Algeria (about 2.3m), Afghanistan (about 2.5m) and Central Asia (about 4m in total).
- The figures most in dispute are Egypt (3m vs 5m), Iran (4.5m within modern borders vs 7.8–10m for the whole Safavid realm) and Tunisia (1m vs 2m). Arabia, Afghanistan and the Caucasus rest on almost no data.

### Cited Findings

**Avakov/Maddison-type series for 1600 within modern borders.** Wikipedia "Demographics of the Middle East" cites Avakov, *Two Thousand Years of Economic Statistics*, Vol. 1, pp. 12–14. Its values for 1600:
- Turkey: Anatolia c.6,500; all of modern Turkey including East Thrace and Istanbul 7,275.
- Iran 4,472; Yemen 2,243; Saudi Arabia 1,809; Syria 1,175; Iraq 1,000.
- Lebanon 292; Oman 275; Jordan 191; Palestine 161; Cyprus 98.
- Kuwait 71; Bahrain 54; UAE 35.
- North Africa: Egypt 5,000; Algeria 2,250; Morocco 2,250; Tunisia 2,000; Libya 500.
- The page gives about 18.5m for the Middle East and about 12m for North Africa. It gives no values for Israel or Qatar.
- [Wikipedia – Demographics of the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_the_Middle_East)

**Wikipedia "List of countries by population in 1600".** Its lead says most figures come from Avakov, who mainly cites Maddison. It also warns that because the source works in modern borders, "the estimates are likely inaccurate". Its values:
- Ottoman entries: Anatolia and East Thrace 7,275; Egypt Eyalet 5,000; Syria 1,175; Iraq 1,070; Palestine 352; Sidon Eyalet 292; Cyprus 98.
- Ottoman vassals: Algiers 2,250; Tunis 1,000; Tripoli 500.
- Safavid Iran 7,800, cited to Dale (2002). Morocco (subdivision) 2,250; Bahrain 54; Arab Emirates 35.
- The Ottoman total is 28,745+. The page gives no rows for the Caucasus, Central Asia or Afghanistan.
- [Wikipedia – List of countries by population in 1600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600)

**Anchor years for interpolation from the same Avakov/Maddison family.**
- 1500: Mamluk Egypt 4,000 [Wikipedia – 1500 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1500).
- 1700 (Wikipedia 1700 list): Anatolia 8,400; Ottoman Egypt 5,400; Safavid Iran 10,000 (cited to Blake 2013); "Morocco and possessions" 8,000 (Avakov) [Wikipedia – 1700 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1700).
- 1800: Egypt Eyalet about 4,000–4,394 [Wikipedia – 1800 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1800).

**McEvedy & Jones 1978, read from the OCR text of the scanned book.** [McEvedy & Jones, Atlas of World Population History (scan, MSU course reading)](https://dmo.econ.msu.ru/Teaching/Histpop/Reading/Atlas%20of%20World%20Pop%20History%20McEvedy&Jones.pdf)
- Cyprus:
  - The population stayed in the 100–200 thousand band from the Iron Age to the mid-19th century.
  - It touched the upper limit during the final Venetian phase in the 16th century, then fell after the Ottoman conquest of 1571.
  - The total was "down to 120,000 by 1600" and "little more than 100,000 in 1650". It stayed there for about 150 years and was nearing 0.2m again by the 1881 British census.
- Russian Turkestan (the five ex-Soviet Central Asian republics, Kazakhstan included):
  - About 2m by AD 500, and "in the period AD 500–1600 the population of Turan doubled again". That implies **about 4m in 1600** for Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Tajikistan and Kyrgyzstan combined.
  - The nomads outnumbered the peasants and town-dwellers "until well on in the 19th century".
  - The book states there is "nothing at all substantial" before the Russian conquest. Even the Khiva and Bukhara figures in the 1897 census were only rounded estimates.
- Transcaucasia:
  - "The traditional figure of 5m for medieval Georgia is absurd". There are "no usable primary data prior to the year 1800".
  - The book's 1600 values for Georgia, Armenia and Azerbaijan sit on a graph that did not survive OCR.
- The Maghreb (Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia):
  - About 5m in AD 1000, "roughly distributed between Morocco, Algeria and Tunisia on a 2:2:1 formula".
  - There was little change over the next 800 years. Recovery from the Black Death "was completed only in the mid 16th century", and was then cut back by the mid-17th-century Mediterranean recession.
  - By 1800 the population was about 6m: Tunisia about 0.8m, Algeria and Morocco about 2.6m each.
  - Russell's 1500 figure for the Maghreb, 3.5m, "is accepted by Braudel".
- Libya:
  - After the Hilali bedouin invasion the population at its low point "cannot have been more than 0.25m".
  - "During the rest of the pre-modern period there was a slow recovery", reaching about 0.75m by the late 19th century.
  - Tripolitania holds about two-thirds of the productive land and probably a similar share of the population.
- Egypt:
  - About 5m was a ceiling, "not exceeded until modern times".
  - In bad spells — the plagues of the 7th and 14th centuries and "the stagnation in the last stage of Ottoman rule" — "the population must have been nearer 3m than 5m".
  - There are "no primary data for the medieval or early modern periods". The French savants estimated 2.5m in 1799, which M&J raise to about 3.5m for 1800, following Abu-Lughod.
  - Medieval estimates are about 3m (Poliak) against 4m (Abu-Lughod; Gibb & Bowen).
- Balkans, for context: by the early 16th century they held 4m Christians and 1m Muslims.

**Ottoman Empire as a whole.**
- 1520: 11,692,000. 1566: 15,000,000. These are presented as extrapolations from tax units [Wikipedia – Demographics of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_the_Ottoman_Empire).
- Barkan's Ottoman-Europe series: about 5 million taxpaying people in 1525–35 and "eight millions" in 1570–80. Braudel, Cipolla and others borrowed the latter.
- Moačanin calls the implied growth (about 1% a year) incredible. He prefers McCarthy's ceiling of 0.5% a year for pre-industrial Turkey and treats about 1580, the peak in the registers, as the safest reference point. [Moačanin, TÜBA 2019](https://www.tuba.gov.tr/files/yayinlar/raporlar/TUBA-978-605-2249-41-3_ch05.pdf)

**Turkey/Anatolia.**
- The tahrir registers show a doubling, sometimes more, of the recorded tax-paying population over the 16th century, in both towns and countryside. Cook (1972) argued this amounted to "population pressure" in parts of rural Anatolia in the second half of the century. [Özel 2004, IJMES 36](https://repository.bilkent.edu.tr/bitstreams/0df8404b-d2c2-45f0-a819-6ee1d5192369/download)
- A search snippet reports Jennings, citing Barkan's table: five major Anatolian provinces grew 59.9%, from 872,610 to 1,360,474, between about 1520/30 and 1571/80. Twelve cities grew from 142,562 to 271,494. The unit is probably households or taxpayers, not persons. I did not verify this against the full text. [Jennings, IJMES](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-journal-of-middle-east-studies/article/abs/urban-population-in-anatolia-in-the-sixteenth-century-a-study-of-kayseri-karaman-amasya-trabzon-and-erzurum/E74E6112471A21B558BBAC9315FD3934)
- Barkan used a multiplier of 5 per household (*hane*); later estate-based work argued for 4 [ResearchGate – Özel abstract page](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/248621809_POPULATION_CHANGES_IN_OTTOMAN_ANATOLIA_DURING_THE_16TH_AND_17TH_CENTURIES_THE_DEMOGRAPHIC_CRISIS_RECONSIDERED).

**Istanbul.** 1500: 200,000; 1550: 660,000; 1700: 700,000. These are Chandler/Lahmeyer-type estimates, and the page notes substantial disagreement among historians [Wikipedia – Demographics of Istanbul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Istanbul).

**Palestine region (Israel plus Palestine).**
- Bachi/DellaPergola: 1533–39 total 156k; 1553–54 total 205k; 1690–91 total 232k.
- Bernard Lewis, from Ottoman registers, gives "about 300,000" around 1550.
- McCarthy considers that in 1601–1801 the population was probably not much smaller than the c.340k of 1850.
- [Wikipedia – Demographic history of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_history_of_Palestine_(region))

**Safavid Iran (whole realm, not modern borders).**
- Willem Floor (*The Economy of Safavid Persia*, 2000): across the 17th century the population was "never higher than nine million in total", and 85–90% of it was rural. Reported in Andrew Newman's BIPS seminar summary. [BIPS – Newman seminar](https://www.bips.ac.uk/?p=3734)
- Blake (*Time in Early Modern Islam*, Cambridge): there are no reliable estimates, but 8–10 million for 1650 "seems reasonable", based on about one-eighth of the land being tillable [Cambridge – Blake chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/time-in-early-modern-islam/safavid-mughal-and-ottoman-empires/9D55F0A0262017473EC8A9A7ED86C508).
- A Russian-language dissertation abstract gives 5–10 million for the 17th–18th centuries. It rejects the high figures of the *Tohfe-yi Shahi* as implausible. [Academia – Population of the Safavid state, autoreferat](https://www.academia.edu/125713715/Population_of_the_Safavid_state_historical_demographic_research_Autoreferat) (search snippet only; the page returned 403)
- Chardin's claim of 40 million for Persia is cited in the Iranica entry on Chardin, and is not accepted [Iranica – Chardin](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/chardin-sir-john/).

**Egypt.**
- The Ottoman Egypt infobox gives 2,335,000 for 1700 [Kiddle mirror of Wikipedia – Ottoman Egypt](https://kids.kiddle.co/Ottoman_Egypt).
- M&J's Egypt passage, quoted above, gives about 3.5m for 1800 and treats the late-Ottoman level as "nearer 3m than 5m".

**Yemen.**
- About 2.6m in 1800, rising to about 3.2m by the end of the 18th century, according to a Gapminder/UN-derived series [Statista – Yemen historical population](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1067153/total-population-yemen-historical/).
- The 1885 Ottoman census put the Yemen vilayet at 2,500,000 [Wikipedia – Yemen vilayet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemen_vilayet).

**Bukhara.**
- Late 19th century: about 2.5–3 million, of whom 10–14% were urban [Encyclopedia.com – Emirate of Bukhara](https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/asia-and-africa/central-asian-history/emirate-bukhara).
- Encyclopaedia Iranica gives 2–2.5 million and notes no census before 1897 [Iranica – Central Asia ii. Demography](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/central-asia-ii/) (search snippet; the page returned 403).

**Maghreb migration.**
- Immigrants may have made up a majority of Algiers' population in 1580–1640 [Cambridge – A History of Algeria, ch. 1](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/history-of-algeria/ecologies-societies-cultures-and-the-state-15161830/6C59DEAD9F70913EC060EDD1465FA6A5).
- The 1609 expulsion of the Moriscos sent "over 300,000" Muslims to the Maghreb. This is a weak, popular source, and the event falls after 1590. [AlgeriaConnect](https://algeriaconnect.com/when-al-andalus-crossed-the-sea-how-centuries-of-migration-helped-reshape-the-maghreb/)

Interpolation notes for 1590 versus 1600:
- **Turkey:**
  - The registers peak around 1575–1585 (Moačanin treats about 1580 as the reference point), so 1590 sits near the peak.
  - The Celali disorders (from 1596) and the Long War (1593–1606) produced large drops in recorded taxpayers by the 1640s in north-central Anatolia (see Key question 2).
  - Use about 7.3m for 1590. A model that wants a 1610 value could take 5–15% off Anatolia. Moačanin's 10–15% loss over 1580–1650 for the Balkans, which he thinks fell less than Anatolia, gives a floor.
- **Iran:**
  - The Safavid figures are for 1600 (Dale) and 1650 (Blake).
  - In 1590 Iran had just ceded Tabriz, western Azerbaijan, Shirvan and Georgia to the Ottomans, and Khorasan was being overrun by the Uzbeks (1588–98). I therefore set 1590 below the mid-17th-century level.
- **Palestine region:** a linear interpolation of Bachi's 205k (1553) and 232k (1690) gives about 212k for 1590. Lewis's 300k pushes the other way, so I use about 240k.
- **Maghreb:** M&J put the end of the post-Black Death recovery in the mid-16th century, so 1590 sits on a plateau. The Morisco and Andalusian inflow (1609–14) comes after the game start.
- **Egypt:** M&J's "nearer 3m" is for the late (18th-century) Ottoman stagnation. Egypt c.1590, after the 16th-century recovery under the Ottomans, can plausibly sit somewhat higher, so I use 3.5m.

## Key question 2: Sub-national distribution c.1590–1600

### Takeaway
Only fragments are firmly documented:
- Ottoman tahrir growth by province, and the 1570s→1640s collapse of recorded taxpayers in north-central Anatolia.
- Istanbul's size, and Palestine's totals.
- Isfahan's size, which is only known later and is disputed.
- M&J's statement that Tripolitania holds about two-thirds of Libya's productive land.

Everything else below is a modelled distribution. It is built from historical administrative structure, settlement ecology and later (19th-century) patterns, and should be treated as adjustable game parameters, not data.

### Cited Findings

**Turkey and Anatolia.**
- Barkan's western Anatolia figures, from a Yörük study:
  - General population growth of 41.74% between 1520–35 and 1570–80.
  - 77,368 rural nomads in the western Anatolian provinces in 1520–30, of whom 52,148 were in military service.
  - [DergiPark – The Yörüks](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/929011)
- Karaman province's rural population nearly doubled between the 1520s and 1570s (Barkan 1970, cited in İnalcık & Faroqhi 1997) [ResearchGate – Özel](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/248621809_POPULATION_CHANGES_IN_OTTOMAN_ANATOLIA_DURING_THE_16TH_AND_17TH_CENTURIES_THE_DEMOGRAPHIC_CRISIS_RECONSIDERED).
- North-central Anatolia, recorded tax-paying adult males (*nefer*), 1560–70s → 1640s, from Özel's Table 1. [Özel 2004](https://repository.bilkent.edu.tr/bitstreams/0df8404b-d2c2-45f0-a819-6ee1d5192369/download)
  - Rural units:

    | Unit | 1560–70s | 1640s | Change |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Amasya kaza | 28,449 | 6,068 | −78.7% |
    | Samsun sancak | 39,609 | 6,617 | −83.3% |
    | Bozok sancak | 41,484 | 4,621 | −88.9% |
    | Harput kaza | 15,379 | 1,476 | −90.4% |

  - Towns: Tokat 3,868 → 3,858; Amasya 2,835 → 1,736; Harput 1,965 → 348.
  - Villages, 1570s → 1640s: Amasya 372 → 228 (−38.7%); Canik 509 → 452 (−11.2%); Bozok 629 → 548 (−12.9%).
  - Özel warns that this "does not necessarily imply that 70–80 percent of the rural population simply died". Part of the drop reflects Celali bands and people evading registration. He still sees a genuine "general demographic crisis" in Anatolia in the first half of the 17th century.
- Celali-era abandonment (c.1596–1610): "in some districts between 30 and 60 percent of rural settlements were temporarily abandoned", in the Konya–Karaman–Niğde–Aksaray–Kayseri area. This is a weak blog source with no named primary. [ardatunca.net](https://www.ardatunca.net/post/demographic-transformation-in-anatolia-and-rumelia-15th-19th-centuries)
- Diyarbekir: van Bruinessen uses Barkan's 1520s registers and a household size of 5, and estimates the city of Diyarbekir at possibly around 50,000. The snippet is garbled. [ResearchGate – Population of Diyarbekir](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/375947966_The_Population_of_Diyarbekir_Ethnic_Composition_and_Other_Demographic_Data)
- Istanbul: 660,000 in 1550 and 700,000 in 1700 [Wikipedia – Demographics of Istanbul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Istanbul).

**Iran.**
- Isfahan, the capital from 1598: "the exact number of its population is not known" [Iranica – Isfahan vii](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/isfahan-vii-safavid-period/).
  - Herbert (1627–29): 70,000 households, about 200,000 people.
  - Olearius (1637): 500,000.
  - Chardin: 600,000, "almost certainly an exaggeration" [Iranica – Safavid dynasty](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/safavids/). Another Iranica entry gives Chardin's figure as about 500,000 [Iranica – Isfahan iii](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/isfahan-iii-population/).
- Safavid Iran was 85–90% rural (Floor) [BIPS](https://www.bips.ac.uk/?p=3734).

**Libya.** Tripolitania contains two-thirds of the productive land and probably a similar share of the population [M&J scan](https://dmo.econ.msu.ru/Teaching/Histpop/Reading/Atlas%20of%20World%20Pop%20History%20McEvedy&Jones.pdf).

**Egypt.** The Nile delta (Lower Egypt) "contains as much productive land as all Upper Egypt" [M&J scan](https://dmo.econ.msu.ru/Teaching/Histpop/Reading/Atlas%20of%20World%20Pop%20History%20McEvedy&Jones.pdf).

**Maghreb.** The ecologically important split is between Morocco, northern Algeria and Tunisia on one side and the Saharan departments of Algeria on the other. Only about 1m km² of the maritime provinces is habitable. [M&J scan](https://dmo.econ.msu.ru/Teaching/Histpop/Reading/Atlas%20of%20World%20Pop%20History%20McEvedy&Jones.pdf)

**Central Asia.** The steppe nomads outnumbered the oasis peasants and town-dwellers until well into the 19th century [M&J scan](https://dmo.econ.msu.ru/Teaching/Histpop/Reading/Atlas%20of%20World%20Pop%20History%20McEvedy&Jones.pdf).

**Palestine.** Muslims were about 93% of the 1553–54 total (188k of 205k) [Wikipedia – Demographic history of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_history_of_Palestine_(region)).

### Inferences

All shares in this section are **modelled (confidence D unless noted)**. Multiply them by the Table 1 best estimate. The shares follow:
- Ottoman and Safavid administrative units and their known relative weight;
- the tahrir evidence that Rum and Karaman (north-central and central Anatolia) were densely settled before the Celali collapse;
- ecology: river valleys, delta and coastal plains against steppe and desert;
- large-city sizes where cited.

## Key question 3: Where the sources disagree, and the most defensible figure

### Takeaway
- The compiler series (Maddison and Avakov) systematically run **higher** than McEvedy & Jones for Egypt and Tunisia, and **lower** for Iran in modern borders.
- Ottoman historians disagree on whether 16th-century "growth" in the tax registers was real or partly a fiscal artefact. This matters more for the trend than for the level at about 1580–90.
- Every compiler figure for Arabia, the Caucasus, Afghanistan and Central Asia is little more than an informed guess.

### Cited Findings
- **General reliability:**
  - Critics say M&J's estimates are "little more than guesses" that do not reflect research since 1978, and that M&J often infer population size from their view of an economy [Long Now – Guinnane on McEvedy & Jones](https://longnow.org/ideas/world-population-guinnane-mcevedy-jones/); [Guinnane, JEH](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-economic-history/article/we-do-not-know-the-population-of-every-country-in-the-world-for-the-past-two-thousand-years/D747DDC6E499C799B0471DBE33FEB0BB).
  - The Wikipedia 1600 table itself warns that Avakov's modern-border figures are "likely inaccurate" [Wikipedia 1600 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600).
- **Egypt:**
  - Avakov/Maddison: 4,000 (1500), 5,000 (1600), 5,400 (1700), about 4,000–4,394 (1800) [1500 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1500); [1600 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600); [1700 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1700); [1800 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1800).
  - M&J: 5m is the never-exceeded pre-modern ceiling, Ottoman stagnation was "nearer 3m than 5m", and 3.5m in 1800. The French savants counted 2.5m in 1799. [M&J scan](https://dmo.econ.msu.ru/Teaching/Histpop/Reading/Atlas%20of%20World%20Pop%20History%20McEvedy&Jones.pdf)
  - The Wikipedia Ottoman Egypt infobox gives 2,335,000 for 1700 [Kiddle/Wikipedia](https://kids.kiddle.co/Ottoman_Egypt).
- **Iran:**
  - Avakov: 4,472 within modern borders [Wikipedia – Demographics of the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_the_Middle_East).
  - Whole Safavid realm: 7.8m c.1600 (Dale) [1600 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600); 8–10m in 1650 (Blake) [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/time-in-early-modern-islam/safavid-mughal-and-ottoman-empires/9D55F0A0262017473EC8A9A7ED86C508); never above 9m in the 17th century (Floor) [BIPS](https://www.bips.ac.uk/?p=3734); 5–10m (autoreferat) [Academia](https://www.academia.edu/125713715/Population_of_the_Safavid_state_historical_demographic_research_Autoreferat).
  - Chardin's 40m is an outlier [Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/chardin-sir-john/).
- **Tunisia:** the same compiler family gives 2,000 [Demographics of the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_the_Middle_East) and 1,000 [1600 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600). M&J give a Maghreb ratio of 2:2:1 and about 0.8m for Tunisia in 1800 [M&J scan](https://dmo.econ.msu.ru/Teaching/Histpop/Reading/Atlas%20of%20World%20Pop%20History%20McEvedy&Jones.pdf).
- **Morocco:**
  - Avakov 2,250 for 1600, but "Morocco and possessions" 8,000 for 1700. The 1600 list's headline for Morocco including the Timbuktu pashalik is 19,000, with subdivisions summing to 13.06m; the table looks garbled. [1600 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600); [1700 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1700)
  - M&J: about 2.6m in 1800 [M&J scan](https://dmo.econ.msu.ru/Teaching/Histpop/Reading/Atlas%20of%20World%20Pop%20History%20McEvedy&Jones.pdf).
- **Cyprus:** M&J 120k (1600) against Avakov 98k [M&J scan](https://dmo.econ.msu.ru/Teaching/Histpop/Reading/Atlas%20of%20World%20Pop%20History%20McEvedy&Jones.pdf); [Demographics of the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_the_Middle_East).
- **Palestine:** Bachi/DellaPergola 205k (1553–54) against Lewis about 300k (c.1550) [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_history_of_Palestine_(region)). The 1600 list's "Palestine 352,000" against Avakov's "Palestine 161" on the other page suggests the two pages use different area definitions [1600 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600).
- **Turkey and the Ottoman trend:**
  - Barkan, followed by Braudel: about 100% growth over the 16th century.
  - Moačanin: tax units are not humans, the growth was partly fiscal, natural growth was capped at about 0.5% a year, and 1580 is the safest reference point. He sees a "moderate decrease … perhaps less in the Balkans than in Anatolia" after 1580 (a 10–15% loss in the Balkans, 25% in the worst case) [TÜBA](https://www.tuba.gov.tr/files/yayinlar/raporlar/TUBA-978-605-2249-41-3_ch05.pdf).
  - Özel: a genuine Anatolian demographic crisis in the early 17th century, but recorded losses of 70–90% overstate actual deaths [Özel 2004](https://repository.bilkent.edu.tr/bitstreams/0df8404b-d2c2-45f0-a819-6ee1d5192369/download).
  - The household multiplier (5 for Barkan, 4 in later studies) alone shifts any persons estimate by 20% [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/248621809_POPULATION_CHANGES_IN_OTTOMAN_ANATOLIA_DURING_THE_16TH_AND_17TH_CENTURIES_THE_DEMOGRAPHIC_CRISIS_RECONSIDERED).
  - Avakov's Anatolia figures, about 6.5m (Anatolia) and 7.275m (all of Turkey) in 1600 and 8.4m in 1700, imply growth through the 17th century. That conflicts with the Ottomanist consensus of an early-17th-century crisis. [Demographics of the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_the_Middle_East); [1700 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1700)
- **Central Asia:** M&J say there is "nothing at all substantial" before the Russian conquest, and that even 1897 figures for Khiva and Bukhara were rounded estimates [M&J scan](https://dmo.econ.msu.ru/Teaching/Histpop/Reading/Atlas%20of%20World%20Pop%20History%20McEvedy&Jones.pdf).
- **Georgia:** M&J call the traditional 5m "absurd" [M&J scan](https://dmo.econ.msu.ru/Teaching/Histpop/Reading/Atlas%20of%20World%20Pop%20History%20McEvedy&Jones.pdf).

### Inferences

Most defensible figure, country by country:

| Country | Range of disagreement | Most defensible for c.1590 | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Egypt | 2.3m (1700 infobox) to 5.0m (Avakov 1600) | **3.0–4.0m; use 3.5m** | M&J's argued ceiling and trough; 18th-century counts at 2.5–3.5m make 5m in 1600 implausible unless Egypt had a 17th-century crash that no cited source documents |
| Iran (modern borders) | 4.47m (Avakov) to about 8m (pro-rated from the Safavid 8–10m) | **about 5.5m** | 1590 is a war low; Floor's ≤9m and Blake's 8–10m are mid-century, whole-realm figures |
| Turkey | 6.0m to 9.0m | **about 7.3m** | Avakov's 7.275m matches a tahrir-peak level once Istanbul and Thrace are included; avoid Avakov's implied 17th-century growth when modelling the trend |
| Tunisia | 1.0m against 2.0m in the same compiler | **1.0m** | M&J's ratio and 0.8m in 1800 |
| Morocco | 2.25m to 8m (1700 "with possessions") | **2.5m** | M&J 2.6m in 1800 with a plateau; 8m includes the Sahelian pashalik and is not usable |
| Algeria | 2.25m to 2.6m | **2.3m** | Sources broadly agree |
| Cyprus | 98k to 120k | **120k** | M&J cite Papadopoullos' Venetian and Ottoman counts (hard data) |
| Israel + Palestine | 205k to 300k (c.1550) | **about 240k** | Midpoint between the Bachi and Lewis series |
| Arabia, Gulf, Caucasus, Afghanistan, Central Asia | no real data | Table 1 values, flagged D | Keep them adjustable for game balance; the Kuwait value of 71k (Avakov) is probably too high |

Implication for the game:
- Treat the D-graded rows and all sub-national shares as tunable parameters.
- Anchor the A/B-graded rows (Turkey, Egypt, the Maghreb, Cyprus, Palestine) to the values above.
- Because the game starts in 1590, model the Anatolian Celali decline (1596–1610) and the Safavid recovery under Abbas I as dynamics, not as starting values.

### Gaps
- I could not see the actual McEvedy & Jones numbers for the Near East chapter, nor the graph values for the Maghreb, Egypt and Transcaucasia. That prevents a row-by-row comparison of M&J against Avakov for most Asian countries.
- I did not obtain Biraben's (1979) regional figures, HYDE 3.x country values (dataset download needed), or Clio Infra values.
- Without the original works or library access I could not check Issawi's (*Economic History of Iran*, *Economic History of Turkey*), Raymond's and Panzac's figures.
- Barkan's own empire-level estimates for c.1580 were not confirmed from a primary or near-primary source; I found only the Ottoman-Europe figures via Moačanin. The 30–35 million sometimes attributed to Barkan for about 1600, and Braudel's 22 million, are therefore left out as unverified.
