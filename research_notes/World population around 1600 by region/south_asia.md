# South Asia: population c.1590–1600, by present-day country and modern first-level region

Scope: India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, Maldives and the Kashmir region. The figures are for c.1600, within present-day borders, in thousands. They are meant to seed a campaign starting on 1 January 1590.

**How to read the tables.** "Cited" means the number appears in a source linked below. "Derived" means this researcher calculated it from cited anchors using the method set out under Key Question 3. The report writer should present derived rows as estimates, not as published figures.

**Confidence grades used in every table:**
- **A**: a published scholarly estimate for this unit and date.
- **B**: derived, but checked against a published estimate for the same area.
- **C**: derived from a cited baseline using a ratio that rests on cited regional evidence.
- **D**: a weak anchor or a judgement call; treat as a placeholder.

## Key Question 1: National totals c.1600 (present-day borders)

### Takeaway
Use **c.142 million** for India, Pakistan and Bangladesh together (undivided pre-1947 India). That is Irfan Habib's cross-checked 1600 figure, which Broadberry and Gupta adopted. It splits roughly as follows:

- India: about 121 million
- Bangladesh: about 11 million
- Pakistan (including Azad Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan): about 9.6 million

Nepal has about 2.75 million (Avakov). Sri Lanka, Bhutan and the Maldives are small and poorly documented: about 0.8 million, 0.15 million and 0.06 million. Kashmir as a whole (both sides of the Line of Control) has about 1.5 million. The all-South-Asia total is about 146 million.

### Cited Findings
- Habib (Cambridge Economic History of India vol. 1, 1982, pp. 164–166) cross-checks the 1600 population of India in three ways:
  - 142 million from cultivated area
  - 144.3 million from land revenue
  - 140–150 million from the size of armies

  Broadberry and Gupta adopt 142 million for 1600 and 1650 (0.00% growth 1600–1650). Their series continues with 164 million (1700), 190 million (1750), 207 million (1801) and 256 million (1871). — [Broadberry & Gupta, "An Anglo-Indian comparison of GDP per capita 1600–1871"](https://web.williams.edu/Economics/seminars/BroadberryGuptaIndiaAndTheGreatDivergence.pdf)
- Broadberry and Gupta put the Mughal Empire's share at 60% of the Indian total. They take this from Moreland and apply it to Habib's 142 million, which gives about 85 million in Mughal territory. — [Broadberry & Gupta](https://web.williams.edu/Economics/seminars/BroadberryGuptaIndiaAndTheGreatDivergence.pdf)
- Shireen Moosvi (The Economy of the Mughal Empire c.1595, 1987, ch. "Population") treats **145 million for 1601** as a reliable estimate. - Moosvi puts India (pre-1947 boundaries) at 140–150 million and **Akbar's empire at about 100 million**. — [SelfStudyHistory summary of the historiography](https://selfstudyhistory.com/2016/01/31/population-of-mughal-india/)

The article states that the figures include the territory of modern Pakistan and Bangladesh. — [Wikipedia, Economic history of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_history_of_India). - **Using these figures for 1590.** Use the 1600 figures unchanged for 1 Jan 1590:
  - Long-run growth was about 0.2% a year (Moosvi to 1871), which would make 1590 about 2% lower.
  - Broadberry and Gupta assume zero growth over 1600–1650.
  - Moreland cut his own estimate to allow for the famine of 1596 (KQ3).

  A decade of normal growth and the famine roughly cancel out, and the difference is far inside the error band. If a separate 1590 number is wanted, multiply by 0.98–1.00.
- **How the 142 million splits between countries (1901 baselines):**
  - India 238.4m (PRB) and Bangladesh 28.9m (Banglapedia).
  - Pakistan in 1901 is about 16.5m by subtraction: 294.4m (1901 total incl. Burma), minus about 10.5m for Burma (from memory, not sourced here), minus India and Bangladesh. This matches the commonly quoted Pakistani series value of about 16.6m for 1901, which was **not verified in this session**.
  - Pre-1947 India in 1901 was therefore about 284m. 142m ÷ 284m = 0.50, so on Habib's and Moosvi's numbers c.1600 is roughly half the 1901 population. Moreland independently says the south c.1600 was "about half" of 1911 (KQ3).
- **Urban population** (useful for city seeding): Habib and Moosvi put the urban share at 15%, which Broadberry and Gupta turn into 21.3m urban in 1600. ## Key Question 2: Subcontinent total c.1600: which estimate is most defensible?

### Takeaway
**Habib's 142 million (1600) is the most defensible single figure.** It comes from three independent cross-checks (cultivated area, revenue and armies) that agree within 140–150 million. It is close to Moosvi's 145 million for 1601 (range 136–150), and economic historians have adopted it (Broadberry and Gupta). Maddison's 135 million is only 5% lower. The older and lower figures rest on assumptions that have been criticised: Moreland's 100 million, Davis's 125 million and Avakov's 113 million. Use 142 million with a plausible range of 125–150 million.

### Cited Findings
- **Moreland (1920), *India at the Death of Akbar*, ch. I §2.**
  - The northern plains from Multan to Monghyr had "well over 30 millions and probably little less than 40 millions".
  - Vijayanagar and the Deccan had "at least thirty millions, and probably substantially more".
  - India as a whole had "somewhere about 100 millions", "only one-third" of the 1911 population of the same area.
  - The south in 1911 had "between sixty and seventy millions", so the south in 1600 was "about half" of 1911.
  - He calls these "first approximations".

  — [Moreland 1920, archive.org full text](https://archive.org/stream/cu31924022895001/cu31924022895001_djvu.txt)
- Moreland's method has two parts:
  - He treated the Ain's *arazi* (measured area) as the gross cropped area. On that basis cultivation c.1600 was about three-quarters of the c.1900 level in the western United Provinces, more than four-fifths in the Doab, and much lower further east.
  - For the south he used army sizes: Vijayanagar mobilised about 650–700k men, and he applied a roughly 1:30 ratio of soldiers to population.

  He made no estimate for Bengal or Gujarat ("populous" or "thickly populated"), nor for Sind, Rajputana, Orissa or Kashmir. — [Moreland 1920](https://archive.org/stream/cu31924022895001/cu31924022895001_djvu.txt); [SelfStudyHistory](https://selfstudyhistory.com/2016/01/31/population-of-mughal-india/)
- Moreland's breakdown is about 60 million for Akbar's empire and 100 million for India. Kingsley Davis (*The Population of India and Pakistan*, 1951) raised the all-India figure to **125 million**. — [SelfStudyHistory](https://selfstudyhistory.com/2016/01/31/population-of-mughal-india/)
- The Cambridge Economic History of India summarises Moreland's method in the same terms: 30–40 million for the plains, 30 million for the Deccan and south, about 100 million in total. — [CEHI, "Population" chapter abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-economic-history-of-india/population/F6993C78D9C4015E7C90FA166481158D)
- Habib's three methods give 142 million, 144.3 million and 140–150 million. — [Broadberry & Gupta](https://web.williams.edu/Economics/seminars/BroadberryGuptaIndiaAndTheGreatDivergence.pdf)
- Moosvi's main assumptions:
  - Cultivated area in 1601 was 50–55% of the area c.1901–1910.
  - The urban to rural ratio was 15:85.
  - Holdings were somewhat larger than in 1901.

  Her results are 136–150 million and 149.07 million, and she settles on 145 million. — [SelfStudyHistory](https://selfstudyhistory.com/2016/01/31/population-of-mughal-india/); [OUP chapter](https://academic.oup.com/book/32718/chapter-abstract/272400288)
- **Ashok Desai (1978)** estimated only **65–95 million** (6.48–9.48 crore) for the Mughal Empire. He divided the total revenue (*jama*) by an estimated land revenue per head. — [SelfStudyHistory](https://selfstudyhistory.com/2016/01/31/population-of-mughal-india/); [Review of Moosvi, academia.edu (search summary)](https://www.academia.edu/21481819/Review_of_S_Moosvi_Economy_of_the_Mughal_Empire)
- Maddison gives 135 million and Avakov 113 million (see KQ1). The Wikipedia Mughal Empire infobox says "c.1600: 150,000,000" without clear sourcing (seen only in a search summary). — [Wikipedia, Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire)
- **Dyson (*A Population History of India*, OUP 2018).**
  - For 1800 Dyson gives about 187 million (secondary summary). — [Socio.Health summary](https://socio.health/population-studies-introduction/population-trends-india-historical-insights/)
  - Dyson stresses that for the period before 1800 "very little is known about the population, and what is thought to be known is fragmentary." — [LSE blog, Dyson](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/internationaldevelopment/2018/10/16/5406/)
  - His own c.1600 figure **could not be retrieved**.

### Inferences
- **Why 142 million over 100–125 million.** Moreland assumed the measured area (*arazi*) equalled the whole cropped area and that the south mobilised 1 in 30 of its people. Both assumptions bias his estimate down. His own text says the south figure is "at least" 30 million and "probably substantially more", and that the figures are first approximations. Habib's three independent checks converge, which is the strongest evidence available.
- **Low-end risk.** The Desai and Avakov range (Mughal empire 65–95 million; India 113 million) is the main reason for a lower bound of about 125 million rather than 135 million. A game wanting a "conservative" world could use 135 million (Maddison), which scales every regional row in KQ3 by 0.95.
- **Internal consistency check.** Applying the regional method in KQ3 (cultivation and population c.1600 roughly half of 1901) to the census baselines gives 147.7 million before scaling. That is independent support for the 140–150 million band.

### Gaps
- Dyson's 1595–1600 estimate and his critique of Habib and Moosvi could not be retrieved: the OUP book, the PDR review (403) and ThePrint (bot wall) were all blocked.
- McEvedy and Jones's subcontinent and regional figures could not be retrieved.
- Habib's CEHI chapter (pp. 163–171) is paywalled, so his figures are known only through Broadberry and Gupta.

## Key Question 3: Sub-national distribution c.1600 (modern states and provinces)

### Takeaway
None of the authors publishes population by every subah. Moosvi gives a combined figure for five core subahs, and Moreland gives a north/south split. The best practical approach is to take the **1901 census within present-day boundaries** and multiply each region by a 1600/1901 ratio of about 0.40–0.80. The ratios are anchored on these points:

- Moosvi's UP acreage data
- Moosvi's five-subah figure
- Moreland's "south about half of 1911"
- Guha's 2–3 million for Ahom
- Pearson's 250,000 for Portuguese Goa

The result is then scaled to 142 million. **Check:** the derived values for Moosvi's five subahs (Agra, Delhi, Lahore, Allahabad, Awadh) sum to about 35.0 million, against her published 35.63 million.

### Cited Findings
- **Moosvi's five-subah figure.** "We can … estimate the population for the five provinces (Agra, Delhi, Lahore, Allahabad and Awadh) on the basis of our data. The total jama of these provinces was 2,125,881,769 dams…". She grosses the jama up by 42.857% to 3,036,970,919 dams, then divides by 85.235 dams of land revenue per head, giving **3.563 crores (35.63 million)** (Moosvi 1987, p. 301). — [Ellis, LSE GEHN WP 14/05](https://www.lse.ac.uk/Economic-History/Assets/Documents/Research/GEHN/GEHNWP14-FE.pdf)
- **Moosvi on Uttar Pradesh.** She reconstructs cultivated acreage for the United Provinces in 1600 and 1910 (pp. 67–69) and finds a **rise of 89% in acreage** between 1600 and 1910. Yields rose by only 8% (weighted), so output rose about 2.04 times. — [Broadberry & Gupta, Table 4](https://web.williams.edu/Economics/seminars/BroadberryGuptaIndiaAndTheGreatDivergence.pdf)
- **Moreland's regional ratios.** Measured cultivation c.1600 relative to c.1900 was about three-quarters in the western UP, more than four-fifths in the Doab, and much lower in the eastern Gangetic plain. The south c.1600 was "about half the present numbers" of 1911 (60–70 million then). — [Moreland 1920](https://archive.org/stream/cu31924022895001/cu31924022895001_djvu.txt)
- **Assam.** Guha estimates the Ahom kingdom at 2–3 million. — [Wikipedia 1600 list citing Guha pp. 26–30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600)
- **Portuguese Goa** had 250,000 people (Pearson). — [Wikipedia 1600 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600)
- **Bengal.** The Wikipedia Bengal Subah infobox gives "1600: 12,919,000", with no method or source shown. — [Wikipedia, Bengal Subah (via search summary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Subah)

### Inferences

**Correction to the StatisticsTimes 1901 table.** The Bihar, Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh figures are in their pre-2000 boundaries: they include Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh and Uttarakhand, which are also listed separately. As printed, the states sum to 250.6m against the census total of 238.4m. The used values are:

- Bihar 21,243,632
- Madhya Pradesh 12,679,214
- Uttar Pradesh 46,647,804

After this correction the sum is 238.37m. That is within 22k of the PRB 238.40m, and the difference is roughly Chandigarh.

**Method.** For each region, 1600 ≈ (1901 present-boundary population) × r, then everything is multiplied by 0.9617 so that India + Pakistan + Bangladesh = 142,000k. The ratio r is set as follows:

## Key Question 4: Where do sources disagree, and which figure is most defensible?

### Takeaway
The main disagreements:

1. **The all-India level.** Moreland, Davis and Avakov give 100–125 million; Habib and Moosvi 140–150 million. Use Habib's 142 million.
2. **The Mughal share.** Estimates run from 56 million (Wikipedia citing CEHI) through 60 million (Moreland), 65–95 million (Desai) and about 85 million (Broadberry and Gupta) to 100 million (Moosvi). Use about 90–100 million for Akbar's empire c.1600.
3. **Bengal's size.** Wikipedia's 12.9 million sits against about 20 million or more implied by the ratio method.
4. **Peripheral countries**, which have almost no scholarly estimates.

### Cited Findings
- Moreland's assumptions are described as "questionable", and Desai's 65–95 million for the Mughal Empire is far below Moosvi's 100 million. — [SelfStudyHistory](https://selfstudyhistory.com/2016/01/31/population-of-mughal-india/)
- A review of Moosvi questions her fixed-yield assumption and cites Desai's 6.48–9.48 crore against her figures. — [academia.edu review (search summary)](https://www.academia.edu/21481819/Review_of_S_Moosvi_Economy_of_the_Mughal_Empire)
- Ellis uses Moosvi's own five-province figure of 35.63m to test her revenue-share claims. — [Ellis](https://www.lse.ac.uk/Economic-History/Assets/Documents/Research/GEHN/GEHNWP14-FE.pdf)
- Broadberry and Gupta treat Habib's three methods as "broadly consistent" with their 142m. — [Broadberry & Gupta](https://web.williams.edu/Economics/seminars/BroadberryGuptaIndiaAndTheGreatDivergence.pdf)
- The Wikipedia 1600 list's "Mughal Empire ~56,000,000" cites CEHI p. 170 together with Avakov's "India 113m, Mughals ruling roughly half". — [Wikipedia 1600 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600)

### Inferences
- **The 56 million Mughal figure is not defensible as Habib's view.** Habib's own total is 142 million. His Mughal-area share, by Moreland's 60% (Broadberry and Gupta) or Moosvi's ratio, is 85–100 million. The 56 million number fits Avakov's 113 × 0.5. Do not use it.
- **Recommended figures for game data:**
  - India + Pakistan + Bangladesh: 142,000k
  - Akbar's empire (South Asian part, c.1595–1600 borders): about 92,000k (this split)
  - Deccan sultanates combined: about 21,000k (Ahmadnagar about 7.4m, Bijapur about 5.5m, Golconda about 8.3m)
  - Vijayanagara and the southern Nayak and Kerala states: about 18,600k
- **Sensitivity.** If the designer prefers Maddison's 135m, multiply every row in S1–S3 by 0.951. For Moosvi's 145m, multiply by 1.021.
- **Which rows are firmest.** UP, the five core subahs and the all-India total are the best-anchored rows (grades A/B). The Deccan and south rest on Moreland's "about half of 1911" together with Habib's total (grade C). Bengal and Bangladesh, the north-east, the Pakistani frontier provinces, Bhutan, the Maldives and Sri Lanka are weakly anchored (grade D).

### Gaps
- Dyson's 2018 assessment, which is the most recent synthesis, was not retrieved. It may argue for lower figures; one search summary hinted that he criticises the high estimates, but this is unconfirmed.
- McEvedy and Jones's regional figures (Ceylon, Nepal, Bhutan) were not retrieved.
- No independent scholarly estimate was found for Sri Lanka, Bhutan, the Maldives or Kashmir c.1600.
