# Population of South-East Asia and Oceania c.1590–1600 (present-day borders, thousands)

Scope notes for the report writer:
- All figures are in **thousands**, within **present-day borders**, for c.1600 unless stated. A 1 January 1590 adjustment is given where the 1590s were unusual (Burma, the Philippines).
- **Confidence grades** used in every table:
  - **A**: a contemporary count, or several independent scholarly estimates that agree within ±20%.
  - **B**: one explicit scholarly estimate for this unit and period (for example a Reid Table 2 row), or a back-projection by a named scholar.
  - **C**: my allocation of a scholarly regional total to modern units, using geography, later censuses and historical context. The total is sourced but the split is not.
  - **D**: weak or no scholarly figure for c.1600. Either a contact-era (1770s–1860s) estimate pushed back by reasoning, or an outright modelling guess.
- ISO 3166-2 codes are given where they are stable. Vietnam merged its provinces in 2025 and Indonesia created new Papuan provinces in 2022, so those rows are keyed to region names as well as codes.
- Search snippets reported that the Reid OCR text is "garbled". A second OCR copy on archive.org reads cleanly and its rows add up to Reid's printed total, so the Reid figures below are reliable.

## Q1. National totals c.1600 (present-day borders)

### Takeaway
South-East Asia (ASEAN-11 territory plus Indonesian Papua) held about **23–24 million** people in 1600. This rests on Reid's 23 million for the region excluding New Guinea, re-allocated to modern borders. The four largest present-day countries were Indonesia (~9.7 M), Vietnam (~5.0 M), Myanmar (~3.1 M) and Thailand (~2.4 M). Oceania is far less certain. Aboriginal Australia (~1 M, range 0.3–4 M) and Papua New Guinea (~1 M, no scholarly figure found) dominate it. Every Pacific island figure is a contact-era (1767–1860) estimate pushed back to 1600, and recent archaeology (lidar, radiocarbon) keeps raising them.

**Other mainland and island figures:**
- Wikipedia's 1600 list gives Lê Đại Việt 5,600 (citing Li Tana 1998, pp. 160–171), Taungoo Burma 5,500 (citing Lieberman 2003, 2014) and Ayutthaya ~2,500 (citing Lieberman 2003, p. 295). Its Avakov figures, said to be on modern borders and mostly Maddison-derived, are Lan Xang (Laos) 319, Cambodia 1,419, the Philippines 791 and the Malay sultanates of Johor, Kedah, Pattani and Perak "<200" combined. It also lists Portuguese Malacca "~195,000", Mrauk U (Arakan) "160,000+", Nan Madol / Saudeleur Pohnpei 25,000 (US NPS) and Rapa Nui 15,000 — [Wikipedia, List of countries by population in 1600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600)
- The same list breaks Burma down as Upper Burma 1.5 M, the Shan highlands 1 M and Lower Burma 0.5 M, while giving a "total of at least 5.5 million" — [Wikipedia 1600 list (via search summary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600)
- Maddison gives Indonesia 10.7 M (1500), 11.7 M (1600), 13.1 M (1700), and "Indochina" 4.5, 5.0 and 5.9 M for the same years — [Wikipedia, Demographic history (Maddison table "Population of Asia 1–1820")](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_history)
- Lieberman: between 1400 and 1820, Vietnam grew from about 2 M to about 7 M, Burma from perhaps 2 M to about 4.5 M, and Siam from a similar start to perhaps 4 M (1820 borders). 1450–1560 and 1720–1820 were the eras of fastest growth — [Lieberman, Strange Parallels vol. 1 (third-party copy, via search excerpt)](https://dokumen.pub/strange-parallels-southeast-asia-in-global-context-c-800-1830-9780521800860-9780511105708-9780521804967.html)
- Newson estimates Luzon and the Visayas at about **1.57 M in 1565**, falling by about two-thirds by the mid-17th century — [Newson, Conquest and Pestilence (publisher summary)](https://muse.jhu.edu/book/8333)
- A review of Newson reports **Luzon 1,004,150 (1570) → 683,760 (1600)** and **Visayas 383,000 (1565) → 221,700 (1600)**. That makes Luzon plus Visayas 905,460 in 1600 — [review on Medium](https://thezoomerhistorian.medium.com/a-review-of-conquest-and-pestilence-in-the-early-spanish-philippines-fa5a60504398). This is a secondary blog source, and its 1565 sum does not match its own components.
- The 1591 Relación de las encomiendas (31 May 1591) counts **166,903 tributantes = 676,612 almas**, excluding the Manila conventual religious. It covers Manila/Tondo, Pampanga, Pangasinan, Ilocos, Cagayán, Laguna, Camarines, Masbate, Cebú, Panay, Balayan and Calilaya. Itemised entries include Manila/Tondo 9,410 tributos (~30,640 souls) and Pampanga 18,680 (74,720 souls) — [Hispanopedia, Encomiendas en Filipinas](https://es.hispanopedia.com/wiki/Encomiendas_en_Filipinas). The document appears in [Blair & Robertson vol. 8 (Project Gutenberg)](https://gutenberg.org/cache/epub/13742/pg13742-images.html). Reid prints 668 for the same count, and the user's brief and some sources give 667,612, so the total is 668–677 thousand depending on transcription.

**Oceania:**
- **Australia.** Bradshaw, Williams, Russell, Ulm & Ingrey (*Nature Human Behaviour*, 16 Sept 2026) estimate a median of **2.22 M in 1788 (likely range ~1–4 M)**. They use radiocarbon dates, genetic effective population size and carrying-capacity models. Their paper describes Radcliffe-Brown's 1930 figure of ~300,000 as built on post-epidemic observations — [UNSW news](https://www.unsw.edu.au/newsroom/news/2026/09/more-than-2-million-indigenous-people-Australia-1788)
- The ABS Year Book 2008 gives Smith's (1980) absolute minimum as **315,000**. It notes other estimates of "over one million" and that archaeology suggests **750,000** "could have been sustained" — [ABS Year Book Australia 2008](https://www.abs.gov.au/ausstats/abs@.nsf/bb8db737e2af84b8ca2571780015701e/68ae74ed632e17a6ca2573d200110075!OpenDocument)
- Butlin's *Our Original Aggression* (1983) radically revised upward the numbers for south-eastern Australia — [catalogue record](https://libcat.library.tamu.edu/Record/in00000530699). His exact figure was not retrieved.
- **Māori.** Te Ara calls ~100,000 in 1769 "the most realistic" European estimate, because it fits feasible growth from settlement in 1250–1300 AD — [Te Ara, Taupori Māori](https://teara.govt.nz/en/taupori-maori-maori-population-change/print). An older Te Ara entry argues the true figure was at least double, because Cook missed the populous inland centres — [Te Ara, early population estimates](https://teara.govt.nz/en/node/171939). Chapple (VUW, 2019) argues the "100,000" is Forster's 1778 figure, not Cook's, and is weakly supported — [VUW](https://www.wgtn.ac.nz/news/2019/01/ongoing-endeavour-to-establish-nzs-1769-population). Easton shows that 85 women and 85 men in 1269 would need about 1.2% p.a. to reach 100,000 by 1769, and that Pool regarded even 0.5% as rapid for antiquity — [Easton](https://eastonbh.ac.nz/2012/09/exercises-in-new-zealands-demography-and-economic-history)
- **Hawaiʻi.** 1778 estimates range from 100,000 to 1,000,000. Stannard (1989) gives 800,000–1,000,000. Dye & Komori (1992) put the peak at ~150,000 around 1450, then a plateau, and give 110,000–150,000 for 1778. Swanson (2019) back-casts 683,200. Kirch attributes a significant 16th–17th century population reduction to chiefly warfare — [Wikipedia, Ancient Hawaiian population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Hawaiian_population). Swanson's exhibit lists 300,000 for Schmitt & Zane (1977), 400,000 for King and 500,000 for Cook's officers — [Swanson, Hūlili 11(2)](https://kamehamehapublishing.org/hulili-11-2-swanson)
- **Tahiti (French Polynesia).** Contact estimates range from 10,000 (Boenechea, 1772) through McArthur (1967) ~30,000–35,000 and Oliver (1974) 35,000, to J.R. Forster 121,500 (1774), Cook 204,000, Rallu 110,000 (2007) and **Rallu ~180,000 (2022/24)** for 1767. The Marquesas were 20,200 in 1842, a post-contact figure — [Rallu, Population and Economics](https://populationandeconomics.pensoft.net/article/116822/download/xml/)
- **Tonga.** Parton & Clark (2022, lidar) estimate **100,000–120,000 pre-contact**, with Tongatapu falling from 50–60k to 10k over 50 years — [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2022-09-reveals-severe-impact-european-contact.html); [ANU](https://www.anu.edu.au/news/all-news/new-data-reveals-impact-of-contact-with-pacific-nations)
- **Samoa.** The mid-19th-century aboriginal population of Western Samoa is estimated at 35,000, and Tutuila (American Samoa) at 3,900 in 1865 — [eHRAF Samoa summary (via search)](https://ehrafworldcultures.yale.edu/cultures/ou08/summary). A 2018 lidar study finds much more extensive inland settlement before the 19th century, implying a larger earlier population — [Samoa News](https://www.samoanews.com/regional/unearthing-samoan-history)
- **Fiji.** McArthur (1967, p. 8) gives ~150,000 before the 1875 measles epidemic — [arXiv paper citing McArthur](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.05100). An 1878 report says the native population was estimated at 200,000 in 1859 — [Papers Past, Globe 19 Aug 1878](https://slbrartest.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/GLOBE18780819.2.20)
- **New Caledonia.** Cook estimated 60,000 on Grande Terre in 1774, and others guessed 20,000 for the Loyalty Islands — [GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/oceania/nc-people.htm)
- **Solomon Islands.** The pre-contact population was "probably in excess of 200,000 and possibly twice or even three times that"; 500,000 is "entirely plausible". The 19th–early 20th century population was 100,000–200,000, and the 1931 census counted 94,066. The same source says archaeologists think Vanuatu's pre-contact population "could have been as high as 700,000" (Huffman 2012) — [Solomon Islands Encyclopaedia](https://solomonencyclopaedia.net/biogs/E000087b.htm)
- **Vanuatu.** About 100,000 c.1870–80, when 25,358 people left in the labour trade — [Wikipedia, History of Vanuatu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Vanuatu). On Aneityum, historical estimates of 3,500–4,000 compare with Spriggs's archaeological 4,600–5,800 pre-contact — [Journal of Pacific History 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00223344.2025.2553514)
- **Marianas (Guam/CNMI).** A 1668 description gives ~180 villages and 35,000–50,000 on Guam. Garcia's 100,000 for the Marianas is considered inflated, with modern estimates of 30,000–60,000. Hezel uses a working figure of 40,000 in 1668. The 1710 census counted ~3,500–4,000 — [Guampedia](https://www.guampedia.com/envisioning-the-past-near-extinction/); [Guampedia essay](https://guampedia.com/?p=1806). The southern Marianas in the 16th–17th centuries have been put at 40,000–100,000, and Guam alone at 3,000–50,000 — [NPS War in the Pacific history](https://www.nps.gov/parkhistory/online_books/npswapa/extcontent/wapa/reed/sectiona-3.htm)
- **Micronesia (FSM).** Pohnpei/Nan Madol (Saudeleur) is given as 25,000 — [Wikipedia 1600 list (citing US NPS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600). Kosrae fell from ~3,000 (1840) to ~300 by 1900. Pohnpei's 1854 smallpox epidemic killed ~4,000, about 40% of the island. Hezel puts 19th-century Micronesian losses at 30–40% overall — [MicSem / Hezel (via search)](https://micsem.org/?p=2087); [Wikipedia, Health in FSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_the_Federated_States_of_Micronesia)
- **New Guinea.** The sweet potato reached the highlands less than 300 years ago — [White & Allen 1980 (Penn archaeobib)](https://pennds.org/archaeobib/items/show/33955). The 1930s highland contacts revealed a "very large population" — [Penn Museum](https://www.penn.museum/sites/expedition/the-kafe/)

Derived totals: SE Asia (11 countries) ≈ **23,600** (Reid 23,000 + ~250 Papua + ~150 Philippines revision + rounding). Oceania (rows above) ≈ **3,900**, of which Australia + PNG ≈ 2,000.

- **Māori back-projection.** A constant 1.2–1.3% p.a. from ~200 people c.1280 gives only ~12k by 1600. That is implausible, because early growth on moa and seal resources was likely faster and later growth slowed after moa extinction (c.1450) and with pā-era warfare. A logistic curve (K ≈ 110k, r ≈ 2%) reaches ~106k by 1769 and ~55k by 1600. I therefore use **50k** with a wide 15–100k range. The 100k ceiling allows for the "at least double" 1769 view. Roughly 85–90% would be in the North Island (kūmara cultivation limit), which is an inference.
- **Hawaiʻi.** Kirch's evidence of 16th–17th century warfare losses and Dye & Komori's plateau both imply no growth between 1600 and 1778, so any accepted 1778 figure can be used for 1590. 300k sits between the archaeological-minimum school and the backcast-maximum school.
- **Australia.** The newest peer-reviewed estimate (2.22 M median) is more than double the long-standing 750k. A game value of ~1.0 M sits inside the 2026 study's range and above the ABS "sustainable" figure. Raise it to ~2 M if you want to follow the 2026 study exactly.
- **1590 vs 1600 adjustments.**
  - **Burma** suffered catastrophic losses in the 1590s: Nanda Bayin's wars, Lower Burma famine and depopulation, and the sack of Pegu in 1599. So 1 Jan 1590 Myanmar is plausibly 5–10% above the 1600 figure, with most of the excess in Lower Burma.
  - **Philippines:** see the note in the table.
  - Everywhere else the 1590–1600 difference is under ±2% and can be ignored.

### Gaps
- The McEvedy & Jones (1978) country figures for 1600 could not be read. Archive.org copies are access-restricted, and the MSU-hosted PDF is a scan with no extractable text. Reid quotes them only for the South Asia and China densities.
- HYDE 3.x country summaries were not consulted, because the data files are off-limits under the no-downloads rule.
- No scholarly 1600 or pre-contact estimate was found for Papua New Guinea, West Papua, Singapore or Brunei. The FSM is covered only for Pohnpei and Kosrae, and the Marquesas only post-contact. These rows are graded D.
- Butlin's actual number (often quoted as ~1.1 M for the continent) was not retrieved, so it is not stated as fact.
- Maddison's separate 1600 figures for the Philippines, Thailand and Burma, and the exact country composition of his "Indochina", were not confirmed.

## Q2. Reid vs. McEvedy & Jones, Maddison and Boomgaard: which is most defensible?

### Takeaway
Reid's 1600 table, a total of 23 M at ~5.5–5.7 persons/km², is the most defensible base. It is region-by-region, explicitly sourced, anchored on dated contemporary counts (1591 Philippine tributes, 1597 Bali, 1614 Kedah, 1631 Mataram, 1687 Siam) and back-projected at a stated 0.2% p.a. Maddison's Indonesia (11.7 M) is about 20% higher than the Reid-derived ~9.7 M. The best-supported revision of Reid is for Java, where revisionists, building on Boomgaard's work, argue that the 1800–1815 anchor Reid used is an undercount. That would push Java, and hence Indonesia, up.

### Cited Findings
- Reid's method: start from the best 1800 estimate and calculate backwards to 1600 at 0.2% p.a. as a base. He uses 0.1% or zero for zones of severe warfare (Cambodia, the Malay Peninsula, Sulawesi), and lets contemporary estimates take precedence where they exist — [Reid, copy A](https://archive.org/details/anthony-john-stanhope-reid-southeast-asia-in-the-age-of-commerce-1450-1680-volume-1-1988)
- Reid: "Taken as a whole, Southeast Asia was sparsely populated in 1600". Its overall density of about 5.5/km² contrasts with ~32 for South Asia and ~37 for China (excluding Tibet), "after McEvedy and Jones 1978", and Europe had roughly double the South-East Asian density — [Reid, copy A](https://archive.org/details/anthony-john-stanhope-reid-southeast-asia-in-the-age-of-commerce-1450-1680-volume-1-1988)
- Critics note that some figures Reid cites, which are crude household counts compiled for taxation, are almost certainly too unreliable for historical use. Lieberman found little evidence in Burma, Siam or Vietnam for Reid's 17th-century "crisis" — [search summary of reviews, incl. ResearchGate "Ages of Commerce in Southeast Asian History"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/372857436_Ages_of_Commerce_in_Southeast_Asian_History); [Brewminate](https://brewminate.com/ages-of-commerce-in-southeast-asian-history/)
- Maddison: Indonesia 11.7 M and Indochina 5.0 M in 1600 — [Wikipedia, Demographic history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_history)
- Boomgaard argues that low growth in Indonesia between 1500 and 1800 is generally assumed, but total stagnation is unlikely — [Boomgaard, "Smallpox, vaccination and the Pax Neerlandica" (JSTOR snippet)](https://www.jstor.org/stable/pdf/27868070.pdf)
- Raffles' 1815 Java count of ~4.6 M is argued to have been really 7–10 M — [IRD paper](https://horizon.documentation.ird.fr/exl-doc/pleins_textes/pleins_textes_7/carton07/34461.pdf). Tsubouchi's re-examination finds under-coverage of regions, households and household members in Raffles' data — [Tsubouchi, Southeast Asian Studies 28 (Kyoto)](https://repository.kulib.kyoto-u.ac.jp/dspace/handle/2433/56403)
- Reid's own Java footnote treats the 3.5 M (1795) and 4.6 M (1815) figures as underestimates, which is why he uses 5.0 M for 1800 — [Reid, copy A](https://archive.org/details/anthony-john-stanhope-reid-southeast-asia-in-the-age-of-commerce-1450-1680-volume-1-1988)
- Van Goens's 1631 Mataram figure, 3.0 M for Central and East Java without the coast, falls to 1.035 M by 1755 (Ricklefs), i.e. −0.85% p.a. — [Reid Table 1, copy B](https://archive.org/details/southeast-asia-in-the-age-of-commerce-1450-1680-the-lands-below-the-winds)
- Lieberman's mainland curve: Vietnam ~2→7 M, Burma ~2→4.5 M, Siam ~2→4 M between 1400 and 1820 — [Lieberman via search excerpt](https://dokumen.pub/strange-parallels-southeast-asia-in-global-context-c-800-1830-9780521800860-9780511105708-9780521804967.html)

### Inferences
- **Mainland.** Reid and Lieberman broadly agree. Reid's Burma 3.1 M, Siam+Isan ~2.3 M and Vietnam ~5.0 M all sit on Lieberman's 1400→1820 trajectories. Reid's figures can be used for the mainland with confidence B.
- **Java.** Interior Mataram alone was put at 3.0 M in 1631 (van Goens), and Reid's own Java total is 4.0 M. Revisionists say Java was 7–10 M in 1815. Back-projecting 7 M at 0.11–0.2% p.a. over 200 years gives ~4.7–5.6 M in 1600. A defensible Java range is therefore **4.0–5.5 M**; keep 4.0 M as the conservative game value. If you want to follow the Maddison/revisionist side, raise Java to ~5.0 M, which makes Indonesia ~10.7 M.
- **Most defensible overall.** Use Reid for 1600 regional totals, Newson for the Philippines (she supersedes Reid's 800 for Luzon+Visayas with a documented 905), and Maddison only as an upper bound for Indonesia. The Wikipedia 1600 list's Maddison/Avakov figures for Laos (319) and Cambodia (1,419) are mechanical modern-border splits. They should not override Reid.
- **McEvedy & Jones.** By Reid's acknowledgement their densities for neighbouring regions are consistent with his, but their SE Asian figures were not verified here.

### Gaps
- Boomgaard's own numerical 1600 estimate for Indonesia or Java was not found. Only his qualitative argument and a third-party 7–10 M figure for 1815 were retrieved.
- McEvedy & Jones's SE Asia and Oceania country rows were not accessible.

## Q3. Sub-national distribution c.1600

### Takeaway
Reid supplies island-level totals for Indonesia and the Philippines, and Newson, the 1591 Relación and a Burma breakdown add some regional anchors. Everything below island or kingdom level is my allocation (grade C/D), guided by contemporary counts (Banten 191k in 1696, Mataram 3.0 M in 1631, Kedah 60k in 1614, Pampanga 74,720 souls and Manila/Tondo 30,640 souls in 1591) and by known 1590s political geography. Java's centre of gravity was Central/East Java, not the west. Vietnam's population was overwhelmingly in the Red River delta and Thanh–Nghệ. Lower Burma was thin. Lan Na and Isan were sparsely settled.

## Q4. Where do sources disagree, and which figure is most defensible?

### Takeaway
Disagreements fall into two kinds. **Scope artefacts**: Burma "5.5 M" is a figure for the Taungoo empire, not modern Myanmar, and Laos 319k versus 1.2M is about whether Isan is included. **Genuine uncertainty**: Java and Indonesia (Reid 4.0/9.7 M vs Maddison 11.7 M), Australia (0.3 → 2.2 M), Hawaiʻi (0.1 → 1 M), Tahiti (30k → 180k) and Tonga (~40k → 100–120k). For the Pacific, recent archaeology (lidar, radiocarbon, demographic backcasting) consistently favours higher figures than the McArthur-school minimalists did.

### Cited Findings
- **Burma.** Taungoo 5.5 M (Lieberman, per Wikipedia), with regional components of 1.5 + 1.0 + 0.5 M — [Wikipedia 1600 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600) — against Reid's 3,100 for Burma — [Reid, copy B](https://archive.org/details/southeast-asia-in-the-age-of-commerce-1450-1680-the-lands-below-the-winds)

### Inferences

| Dispute | Most defensible for a 1590 start | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Burma 3.1 M vs 5.5 M | **3.1 M** (Myanmar borders) | The 5.5 M covers the Taungoo empire's wider sphere (Lan Na, other Shan/Tai lands). Its own regional components sum to ~3.0 M + Arakan, which matches Reid. |
| Laos 0.32 vs 1.2 M | **~0.75 M Laos + ~0.45 M Isan** | Reid's 1.2 M includes Isan and is based on Lao enumerations. 319k looks like a mechanical Maddison-style share. |
| Cambodia 1.23 vs 1.42 M | **~0.93 M** | Reid's 1.23 M already includes Champa and the delta, which lie in modern Vietnam. Avakov's 1.42 M is unexplained. |
| Vietnam 4.7–5.6 M | **~5.0 M** | Reid N+C 4.7 M + ~0.3 M Champa/delta; Li Tana's 5.6 M is the upper side. |
| Indonesia 9.7 vs 11.7 M | **~9.7 M (raise to ~10.7 M if Java = 5 M)** | Reid is region-by-region and documented. Maddison is a top-down series. Java is the only island with a strong argument to go higher. |
| Philippines 0.79–1.05 M | **~1.05 M (1600); ~1.15–1.2 M (1590)** | Newson's archival reconstruction supersedes Reid's rounder 800. The tribute counts are a floor that excludes unpacified areas. |
| Malacca 195k | **Reject; use ~30k for Melaka state** | Inconsistent with Reid's whole-peninsula 500k and Avakov's <200k for four sultanates. |
| Australia 0.3–2.2 M | **~1.0 M** (go to ~2 M if following the 2026 study) | The older low figures are acknowledged as post-epidemic undercounts, and the 2026 range's lower bound is ~1 M. |
| Hawaiʻi 0.1–1 M | **~0.3 M** | Kirch and the plateau model imply no 1600–1778 growth. 300k sits between the archaeological minimum and backcast maximum schools. |
| Tahiti / Society Is. 30–180k | **~100k** (Society Is.) | Rallu's revisions are the most recent formal demographic work, but his own range has moved (70k → 110k → 180k). Pick the middle. |
| Tonga 40k vs 100–120k | **~100k** | The newest lidar study is cross-checked against shipping and missionary data. |
| Vanuatu 0.1–0.7 M | **~0.2 M** | 700k is an upper bound. Aneityum's archaeological ~25–45% uplift over historical estimates applied to a ~150k early-19th-century level gives ~200k. |
| Marianas 40k vs 100k | **~50k** (Guam 30k + CNMI 20k) | Modern consensus is 30–60k. 100k is regarded as inflated. |

### Gaps
- No direct scholarly 1600 figure exists for any Pacific island group except Pohnpei (25k) and Rapa Nui (15k, outside scope). All other Pacific values are contact-era figures assumed flat to 1600. That flat assumption is reasonable where archaeology indicates a pre-contact plateau (Hawaiʻi, Tonga), but it is untested elsewhere.
- The disagreement between Reid and Lieberman about Reid's "17th-century crisis" affects post-1600 trajectories (for the game's growth model) more than 1600 levels. Lieberman's 1600-specific numbers for each kingdom were seen only through Wikipedia citations, not in his text.
