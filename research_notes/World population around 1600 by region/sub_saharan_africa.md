# Sub-Saharan Africa (incl. Sudan and the Horn): population c.1590–1600 by present-day country and sub-national region

Units: thousands of people (k), present-day borders, unless stated otherwise. Target date: 1600, plus a derived 1 January 1590 value for the game start.

Confidence grades used in every table:
- **A**: well documented. In practice this only covers islands that were uninhabited in 1590.
- **B**: a model or compilation figure that is partly cross-checked by documentary scholarship on a polity inside the country (e.g. Thornton on Kongo, Elphick on the Khoikhoi).
- **C**: a model or compilation figure (HYDE 3.3, or the McEvedy–Jones/Maddison lineage) that looks plausible against the 1950 population but has no independent check.
- **D**: the compilation figure is anomalous or strongly contested, so the best estimate here is my own correction. Treat it as a game-design value.

## Key question 1: national totals c.1600 (present-day borders) for every listed country

### Takeaway
No scholarly source gives a full, documented country-by-country count for Africa in 1600. The only complete present-day-border series available online is HYDE 3.3, shown through Our World in Data; for most of Africa it is a top-down allocation. Maddison (2010) covers only a handful of African countries before 1820. The table below therefore takes HYDE 3.3 as the backbone, cross-checks it against Maddison, the 1950 UN population and polity-level scholarship, and corrects clear HYDE anomalies: Benin, Sierra Leone, Gambia, Guinea-Bissau, Senegal, São Tomé, Ethiopia and Eritrea. The corrected best estimates sum to about **58.5 million for c.1600**, almost exactly HYDE's own total of 58.4 million, and to about **57.7 million on 1 January 1590**.

### Cited findings
- HYDE 3.3 (2023) is OWID's source for all country populations before 1800. Gapminder covers 1800–1949 and UN WPP 2024 covers 1950 onward. Before 1800 OWID takes continental aggregates directly from HYDE — [OWID population data page](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population?tab=table&time=1600&region=Africa)
- HYDE 3.3 country values for 1500, 1600 and 1700 (columns 2–4 of the table below) were read from the rendered OWID tables — [OWID table, 1600](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population?tab=table&time=1600&region=Africa); [OWID table, 1500 to 1700](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population?tab=table&time=1500..1700)
- The 1950 values (UN WPP 2024) were read from the same OWID series — [OWID table, 1950](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population?tab=table&time=1950)
- Maddison 2010 values for 1600 by country, as reproduced on Wikipedia: Ethiopia 2.3M, Mozambique 1.3M, South Africa 0.7M, "Sudan (former)" 4.2M. South Africa's row is complete (0.1/0.3/0.6/0.7/1.0M for years 1/1000/1500/1600/1700). The Ethiopia, Mozambique and Sudan rows have two blank cells that the extracted text could not place; my column alignment for those three rows is an inference — [Wikipedia: Estimates of historical world population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estimates_of_historical_world_population)
- Avakov's compilation, which relies mostly on Maddison, gives the "Ethiopian Empire" 2,104,000 in 1600 and the "Kingdom of Kongo" 2,000,000 (citing Heywood 2009, *JAH*). It assigns the "Pashalik of Timbuktu" 10.8M by applying an area-times-density method. The page itself warns that its modern-border calculations are likely inaccurate — [Wikipedia: List of countries by population in 1600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600)
- Kongo: Thornton (1977) used Capuchin baptism statistics and a reconstructed age structure to estimate about 500,000 for 1650–1700, against a "commonly accepted" 2 million — [Thornton 1977, JAH abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-african-history/article/abs/demography-and-history-in-the-kingdom-of-kongo-15501750/DDA0D0AD06657B5C47D10952B7B4585B). His exact figures were 509,250 in 1650 and 532,000 in 1700 — [Cahiers d'études africaines](https://journals.openedition.org/etudesafricaines/18367). He later revised the mid-17th-century figure to about 790,000 and described it as an order of magnitude — [Thornton, "Revising the Population History of the Kingdom of Kongo"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-african-history/article/revising-the-population-history-of-the-kingdom-of-kongo/7CF3BDE8A2CB2D4EE26BD79644143A8F). Matonda Sakala (2016) disputes the baptism method and argues the Inkisi basin was dense — [ULB record](https://difusion.ulb.ac.be/vufind/Record/ULB-DIPOT:oai:dipot.ulb.ac.be:2013/247744/TOC)
- Angola: the Portuguese census of 1777 counted 474,117 inhabitants in the colonial "Reino de Angola", which covered only the Portuguese-controlled districts — [Menz & Lopes 2017, Revista de História](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/2850/285063007006/285063007006.pdf)
- Ethiopia: Wikipedia's CSA-sourced table gives 6,000,000 for 1500 (no 1600 or 1700 rows), and the article says the population was "only about nine million" in the 19th century (Clarence-Smith 1989) — [Wikipedia: Demographics of Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Ethiopia)
- South Africa (Khoisan west): Elphick & Malherbe (1989) estimate about 50,000 Khoikhoi in the whole south-western Cape in 1652. Published estimates for that region range from 11,000 to 200,000, a factor of 19 — [La Croix, "The Khoikhoi Population, 1652–1780"](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hai/wpaper/201802.html); [working paper PDF](https://www.economics.hawaii.edu/research/workingpapers/WP_18-02.pdf)
- Mauritius was uninhabited until the Dutch settled it in 1638, although Dutch ships visited from 1598 — [CIA World Factbook (2023 archive)](https://www.cia.gov/the-world-factbook/about/archives/2023/countries/mauritius); [Wikipedia: History of Mauritius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Mauritius)
- Seychelles stayed uninhabited until the first settlers arrived on the *Thélemaque* on 27 August 1770 — [Wikipedia: History of Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Seychelles)
- Saint Helena was found uninhabited by the Portuguese, who made no permanent settlement. The English East India Company settled it in 1659 under John Dutton. Sources disagree on whether the Dutch occupied it before then — [West Africa Study Circle: St Helena history](https://wasc.org.uk/WASC_hist_StH.html); [Wikipedia: Jamestown, Saint Helena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamestown,_Saint_Helena)
- São Tomé: the white population never exceeded 1,000 and was under 200 by 1600. The enslaved population was much larger than on the other Portuguese Atlantic islands; there were revolts in 1574 and 1586, and Amador's rising of 1595–96 nearly took the island. Sugar declined from the 1580s — [World History Encyclopedia: Portuguese colonisation of São Tomé and Príncipe](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/1763/the-portuguese-colonization-of-sao-tome-and-princi/)
- Cape Verde: no 16th-century count was found. Wikipedia's history article has no 16th- or 17th-century figures; Fogo fell from 5,700 to 4,200 around 1777 — [Wikipedia: History of Cape Verde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Cape_Verde)
- Songhai (Mali/Niger): Seshat notes that substantiated population estimates for the Songhay empire are hard to find, and cites 70,000 people in Timbuktu by about 1580 — [Seshat: Songhay](https://seshat-db.com/core/polity/242). Other popular sources give Timbuktu anywhere from 25,000 to 100,000 and Gao 50,000–100,000 — [World History Encyclopedia: Songhai Empire](https://www.worldhistory.org/Songhai_Empire/)
- Sudan's 1955/56 census enumerated 10.1M (adjusted to 10.3M) in the former Sudan, which included today's South Sudan — [Al-Neelain University paper](https://mail.neelain.edu.sd/papers/PbcSC3rgaEMyU9HA6Iti4cHRXgkMK1mHtaLhcMyQ.pdf)

## Key question 2: Africa's total c.1600, how it splits between West, Central, East and Southern Africa, and which figure is most defensible

### Takeaway
The "McEvedy & Jones ~55 million" figure in the brief is almost certainly the **whole-continent** total. The Maddison series, which follows the same lineage, gives Africa about 55.3M for 1600, of which **sub-Saharan Africa is 44.3M** and North Africa 11.0M. HYDE 3.3 gives about **58.4M for sub-Saharan Africa** (my sum of its country values, including Sudan and Mauritania). Manning's simulations put the continent at about 140M in 1650–1870, and Diop-Maes's 600–800M for 1500 is an outlier. The most defensible working figure for a game is **about 58M (range about 45–90M)** for SSA including Sudan and the Horn. The split is about 43% West, 20% Central, 35% East (with Sudan and the Horn at about 17% of the total on their own) and under 2% Southern.

### Cited findings
- Maddison (2010 database) gives sub-Saharan Africa 38.3M in 1500, 44.3M in 1600 and 51.8M in 1700. The regional row continues with 63.3M in 1820 and 70.0M in 1870 — [Wikipedia: Estimates of historical world population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estimates_of_historical_world_population)
- Maddison/Avakov North Africa in 1600: Egypt 5.0M, Algiers 2.25M, Tunis 1.0M, Tripoli 0.5M, Morocco 2.25M, making 11.0M — [Wikipedia: List of countries by population in 1600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600). HYDE 3.3 gives the same five values for 1600 — [OWID table, 1600](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population?tab=table&time=1600&region=Africa)
- Manning's baseline continental estimate is about 140 million during the high tide of the oceangoing slave trades (ca. 1650–1870), the upper end of the spectrum. His figures are the only ones built on simulation models fed with Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database exports. Frankema argues that the models leave out New World crops (maize, cassava), so they understate natural growth, and that the 140M figure is doubtful — [Frankema 2019, "Africa and the Demographic Consequences of the Columbian Exchange"](https://edepot.wur.nl/471136)
- Manning's 2014 chapter finds the African population in the 17th–20th centuries larger than previously thought and growing more slowly — [Manning 2014, Cambridge chapter abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/africas-development-in-historical-perspective/african-population-16502000-comparisons-and-implications-of-new-estimates/B7D9C5658C2618070CCFDA39354914BA)
- 1850 benchmark: Manning puts total Africa at 139.6M and Frankema & Jerven at 114.1M (their Table 10). The latest Frankema–Jerven dataset has 106M for 1850, closer to Willcox and Carr-Saunders — [Frankema & Jerven 2014, EHR](https://ewoutfrankema.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Reconstructing_African_Population.EHR2014.pdf); [Frankema 2019](https://edepot.wur.nl/471136)
- Manning puts West Africa at "perhaps 20 to 25" million in 1450 — [Manning, "Slavery and slave trade in West Africa 1450–1930" (AEHN)](https://www.aehnetwork.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/Manning.Slavery-and-Slave-Trade-in-West-Africa-1450-1930.pdf)
- Diop-Maes estimates sub-Saharan Africa at 600–800 million around 1500/1550, or 30–40 per km², falling to about 200 million by 1850–70. French Wikipedia calls this a high hypothesis and contrasts it with Bairoch (80 million in the 16th century) and INED (70–90 million at the end of the 15th century). These are reported from search-result excerpts of the ANKH article and of fr.wikipedia; I could not open the ANKH page because of a certificate error — [ANKH (Diop-Maes)](http://www.ankhonline.com/revue/diop_lm_histoire_evolution_population_afrique.htm); [fr.wikipedia: Commerce triangulaire](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commerce_triangulaire)
- Diop-Maes's 1985 *Population* article sets out to cross-check travellers' accounts with archaeology. In the same issue Biraben criticised her reliance on travellers and merchants who may have exaggerated, and noted that earlier continent-wide estimates favoured a "relatively moderate" population — [Persée: Diop-Maes 1985](https://www.persee.fr/doc/pop_0032-4663_1985_num_40_6_17566); [Persée: Biraben comment](https://www.persee.fr/doc/pop_0032-4663_1985_num_40_6_17567)
- OWID builds pre-1800 continental values directly from HYDE 3.3 — [OWID population data page](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population?tab=table&time=1600&region=Africa)
- Low-quality aggregator figures exist and were excluded: johnstonsarchive gives Africa 85M in 1500 and 120M in 1700 with no 1600 value and no source tied to the figures — [johnstonsarchive](https://johnstonsarchive.net/other/worldpop.html)

- **Why about 58M is defensible.** Maddison's SSA series (63.3M in 1820, 70.0M in 1870) sits about a third below Frankema–Jerven's 1850 continental 106–114M, even after allowing for North Africa. I assume North Africa was roughly 12–15M in 1850; I did not source that. Scaling Maddison's 1600 figure by the same factor gives about 60–66M. Equally, about 100M for SSA in 1850 discounted at 0.2 %/yr over 250 years gives about 60M. Both routes land near the HYDE total. Manning's 140M needs near-zero growth from 1650 to 1850 and omits the boost from New World crops (Frankema's critique), so it is better treated as an upper bound.

## Key question 3: sub-national distribution c.1600 (Nigeria, Ethiopia, DR Congo, Sudan, Tanzania/Kenya, Mozambique, South Africa, plus Somalia and Angola)

### Takeaway
No historical source gives sub-national counts for 1600. The only firm anchors are:
- Nigeria's colonial censuses, which put the North at 50–57% of the total;
- Thornton's Kongo estimate of 0.5–0.8M for the whole kingdom;
- Elphick's figure of about 50k Khoikhoi in the south-western Cape in 1652.

Everything below is therefore a **reasoned allocation (confidence D)**. It is built from those anchors, the polity map of 1590 and modern density patterns, and is meant to be applied to the national best estimates in Q1.

### Cited findings
- Nigeria's census totals by North/South, in millions: 1911 N 8.12 / S 7.93 (total 16.05); 1921 N 10.56 / S 8.16; 1931 N 11.44 / S 8.62; 1953 N 16.84 / S 13.58 (total 30.42). ## Key question 4: where the sources disagree, and which figure is most defensible

### Takeaway
The disagreements are large. At continental scale they span an order of magnitude, from about 44M (Maddison) to 600–800M (Diop-Maes). At country scale they run to factors of 2–3 (Kongo, Ethiopia) and to obvious data errors in HYDE (Benin, Sierra Leone, Gambia). The most defensible approach is a **middle "HYDE-scale" total (about 58M) with country-level corrections**. It is about a third above the Maddison/McEvedy–Jones lineage, which Manning and Frankema–Jerven both suggest is too low, and well below Manning's simulated about 140M for the continent and Diop-Maes's figures.

### Cited findings
- **Continental scale.** Maddison puts SSA at 44.3M in 1600 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estimates_of_historical_world_population). Manning's continental baseline is about 140M for 1650–1870 — [Frankema 2019](https://edepot.wur.nl/471136). Diop-Maes gives 600–800M for c.1500 and Bairoch 80M, while Biraben's critique favours moderate figures — [fr.wikipedia: Commerce triangulaire](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commerce_triangulaire); [Persée: Biraben](https://www.persee.fr/doc/pop_0032-4663_1985_num_40_6_17567)
- **The 1850 anchor.** Manning has 139.6M and Frankema–Jerven 114.1M or 106M (latest). Frankema & Jerven argue that Manning's Indian-census-based default growth rate double-counts famine effects and does not fit North or Southern Africa — [Frankema & Jerven 2014](https://ewoutfrankema.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Reconstructing_African_Population.EHR2014.pdf); [Frankema 2019](https://edepot.wur.nl/471136)
- **Kongo.** Thornton gives about 509k (1650), later revised to about 790k; the older consensus is 2M (Heywood 2009, per the Avakov list); Matonda Sakala criticises Thornton's method — [Thornton 1977](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-african-history/article/abs/demography-and-history-in-the-kingdom-of-kongo-15501750/DDA0D0AD06657B5C47D10952B7B4585B); [Thornton revision](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-african-history/article/revising-the-population-history-of-the-kingdom-of-kongo/7CF3BDE8A2CB2D4EE26BD79644143A8F); [Wikipedia 1600 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600); [ULB](https://difusion.ulb.ac.be/vufind/Record/ULB-DIPOT:oai:dipot.ulb.ac.be:2013/247744/TOC)
- **Ethiopia.** Avakov has 2.10M (1600), Maddison 2.3M and HYDE 2.355M (flat 1500–1700). CSA gives 6M (1500), and the population was about 9M in the 19th century (Clarence-Smith) — [Wikipedia 1600 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600); [Wikipedia: Estimates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estimates_of_historical_world_population); [OWID HYDE](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population?tab=table&time=1500..1700); [Wikipedia: Demographics of Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Ethiopia)
- **Khoikhoi.** Estimates for the south-western Cape in 1652 vary 19-fold, from 11k to 200k; 50k is the usual benchmark — [La Croix](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hai/wpaper/201802.html)
- **Songhai cities.** Timbuktu is put at 25k–100k (70k by c.1580 in Seshat's cited figure) and Gao at 50k–100k. Empire-wide totals are unsubstantiated — [Seshat](https://seshat-db.com/core/polity/242); [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/Songhai_Empire/)
- **HYDE 3.3 country anomalies** (1600):
  - Benin: 3.10M, rising to 6.26M by 1700, against 2.25M in 1950.
  - Sierra Leone: 7.4k, against 2.0M in 1950.
  - Gambia: 4.0k, against 0.31M in 1950.
  - Ethiopia: flat 2.355M in 1500, 1600 and 1700.
  - South Sudan: no 1500 value, then 0.86M in 1600.

  Sources: [OWID 1600](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population?tab=table&time=1600&region=Africa); [OWID 1500–1700](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population?tab=table&time=1500..1700); [OWID 1950](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/population?tab=table&time=1950)
- **Nigerian censuses** are politically distorted. The 1952/53 count may have been undercounted by up to 28%, which affects any back-projection — [Frankema & Jerven 2014](https://ewoutfrankema.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Reconstructing_African_Population.EHR2014.pdf)
- **Avakov's "Pashalik of Timbuktu" figure of 10.8M** is an area-times-density construction, and the page warns that its estimates are likely inaccurate — [Wikipedia 1600 list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_1600)

### Inferences — most defensible choices, by issue
1. **Continental total.** Use about 58M for SSA (1600), within a range of about 45–90M.
   - Reject Diop-Maes. Her figures would imply densities near Europe's, built on travellers' reports that Biraben shows to be unreliable.
   - Treat Manning's about 140M (continent) as an upper bound.
   - Treat the 44M of Maddison and McEvedy–Jones as a lower bound.
2. **Kongo.** Use Thornton's revised order of magnitude, 0.6–0.8M for the kingdom (Angolan and DRC parts together), not 2M. Thornton's figure is the only one built from documented baptism data; the 2M figure is an older consensus. The allocations above give about 800k for the Kongo-region provinces in total, so scale them down slightly if the game models Kongo's own territory.
3. **Ethiopia.** Use 4.5M (range 2.1–6.5M). The lineage figure of about 2.3M implies an unrealistically fast 19th-century jump to the CSA/Clarence-Smith levels. CSA's 6M for 1500 has no methodology, and the Adal war and Oromo migrations (1529–1600) argue against the top of the range. **Weakest major row: strong disagreement.**
4. **Sudan.** Use Sudan 3.3M plus South Sudan 0.85M, about 4.15M together. This matches Maddison's former-Sudan figure of about 4.2M (if the column alignment is right) and avoids HYDE's apparent double count of 5.16M.
5. **HYDE errors in West Africa.** Replace Benin, Sierra Leone, Gambia, Guinea-Bissau and Senegal with ratio-based values, using a 1600/1950 ratio of about 0.28–0.33. These are grade D.
6. **Nigeria.** Keep HYDE's 14M. Its ratio to 1950 (0.38) matches neighbouring countries, and Nigeria's share of West Africa (about 55%) is consistent with the 20th-century share. Use a range of 8–20M.
7. **South Africa.** Use 0.65M (HYDE 0.60M, Maddison 0.70M), with about 100k Khoisan, of whom about 50k are Cape Khoikhoi.
8. **Islands.** Mauritius, Seychelles and Saint Helena are 0 (grade A). Cape Verde is about 12k, São Tomé and Príncipe about 12k and Comoros about 30k (grades C/D).

### Gaps
- I did not verify McEvedy & Jones's own African country and regional figures (book not accessible), nor Manning's 1650 regional table (PDF blocked).
- No polity-level documentary estimates were found for Bornu, the Hausa states, Oyo, Benin (Edo), Mutapa, the Swahili coast as a whole, or Madagascar c.1600.
- The Cambridge History of Africa vol. 4 and Clio Infra were not consulted: no accessible pages were found, and Clio Infra's population series is a downloadable dataset, which the brief excluded.
- An aggregator figure of 87M (1500) / 113M (1600) for Africa (thewrap.com) has no named source and was excluded.
